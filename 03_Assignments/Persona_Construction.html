The assignment

Later in the semester you will build and test an original AI reviewer persona for your own component. The catalog is large and it will still never cover everything. Constructing one that works — and demonstrating that it found something the existing ones did not — is the real proof that you understand the technique rather than just following instructions. You will submit: the persona prompt, what it returned, what the catalog personas missed that it caught, and how you iterated on the prompt to get there. It carries an AI Review Log like everything else — and on this one, field 5 is about a persona you wrote, so critiques rejected means the places your own reviewer was wrong.

Why this one is separate

Every other AI task this semester runs a persona somebody else wrote. This is the one where you write it, and it is the only assessment in the course that cannot be produced by following instructions — which is exactly why it is graded on its own rather than folded into a participation score.

One answer is already published, and it does not count

You will meet A Spy's Guide to Running AI (not included in the public copy — a third-party document, shared with this class under a permission that does not extend to redistribution) in Week 8. In it, John O'Neil, Jim Lawler and Mike Mears describe something the persona catalog genuinely does not contain: a reviewer whose job is to decide when to stop trusting the model altogether. They give four conditions for it. They also say, correctly, that nobody has written this part down yet. That is now written down, so building it is no longer original. A persona assembled out of their four conditions is a good persona and it is not your work, and this assignment is the one place in the course where that distinction is the entire grade. It is told to you rather than hidden from you for a reason. The catalog is large, an essay just added one more to it in front of you, and there are still gaps — because your component is not like anyone else's. Knowing where somebody else already dug is how you avoid digging there. If you use their idea as a starting point, say so and show what you added; an acknowledged debt with something built on top of it is worth far more here than a rediscovery you cannot prove was yours.

How this is assessed

Against the Persona Construction rubric, out of 10 points. Submit the persona prompt, what it returned, what the catalog personas missed that it caught, and how you iterated to get there.

What you turn in

This is the complete list. If it is not here it is not required, and if it is here it is not optional. Everything goes in one submission.

  1. Your AI Review Log. The same five fields every time, listed below.
  2. The transcript of your AI conversation. The whole thing, saved and attached - not a summary of it and not the parts that went well. If you used more than one tool or more than one session, attach all of them.

The AI Review Log, field by field

Required fields of the AI Review Log
Tool(s) usedWhich AI tool(s), e.g. TAMU AI Chat (Claude Sonnet), M365 Copilot.
Personas runWhich reviewer personas you used, and the prompt you gave each.
Top critiquesThe three strongest critiques each persona raised.
Changes madeWhat you actually changed in response, and where.
Critiques rejectedWhat you chose not to act on, and why.

The “critiques rejected” field carries the most weight. Accepting every suggestion an AI makes is not review — it is transcription. Show us where you overruled it, and why you were right to.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Open the conversation by saying who you are and what the rule is. Paste this before anything else: "I am a student at Texas A&M University and I am bound by the Aggie Honor Code. This is not a writing assignment for the W designation, so no rule about whose words these are applies. For this assignment I MAY use AI to generate words that I turn in. I am required to save and submit the transcript of this conversation with my assignment. Restate those rules back to me in your own words before we start." Asking it to restate rather than to agree is the point: a model that has misread the rule shows you on the first turn.
  2. Submit the transcript. Save the whole conversation and hand it in with the assignment. It is not a formality and it is not only for catching people - it is the evidence behind the AI Review Log, and where the log says what you rejected, the transcript shows what you were actually offered.
