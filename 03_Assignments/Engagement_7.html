The assignment

Engagement is checked nine times during the semester, on Thursdays, and each check is worth 10 points in its own gradebook item. Engagement is not attendance. Attendance means you were in the room; engagement is whether the work of the session actually got done by you. Both are graded, because on a team project your absence costs other people time. The tenth item in this part of the grade is the Persona Construction activity, which has its own page and its own deadline.

What counts

  • Studio participation — present, working, and producing the week's checkpoint artifact.
  • Honest reporting to your Project Manager — including problems, early. A component that is behind is a project risk; a component that is behind and hidden is a project failure.
  • Peer reviews delivered on time and adding something beyond what the AI produced.

What you turn in

A short account of the session — half a page, text entry or a file. Three things, and no more: This is not a reflection and it is not an essay. It is the evidence that the work of the session got done by you, written down so it can be marked against the rubric. Half a page is plenty; three sentences is often enough.

This is the complete list. If it is not here it is not required, and if it is here it is not optional. Everything goes in one submission.

  1. What you produced. The checkpoint artifact you worked on, and where it is.
  2. What review you gave. Who you reviewed, and the one thing you told them that they could act on.
  3. What you reported to your Project Manager, including anything that is behind.
  4. If you used AI on this, the transcript of that conversation. AI is not required here. If you did use it, the whole conversation comes in with the work - not a summary of it, and not only the parts that went well.

How this is assessed

Against the rubric, on what you hand in and on what your Project Manager's status memos record. The account you write is the artifact the rubric is applied to — without it there is nothing to mark, which is exactly the problem this replaced. Engagement previously had nothing to upload, which made it impossible for a student to see how it was graded and impossible for the instructor to apply the rubric to anything. If you are reading an older page that says there is nothing to submit, this one is right.

If you use AI on this, two rules apply

This is not one of the assignments that requires AI, and nothing here obliges you to open a tool. But the two rules below hold everywhere in this course, including here: if you use AI on something you hand in, you announce yourself first and you hand in the transcript with it.

  1. Open the conversation by saying who you are and what the rule is. Paste this before anything else: "I am a student at Texas A&M University and I am bound by the Aggie Honor Code. This is not a writing assignment for the W designation, so no rule about whose words these are applies. For this assignment I MAY use AI to generate words that I turn in. I am required to save and submit the transcript of this conversation with my assignment. Restate those rules back to me in your own words before we start." Asking it to restate rather than to agree is the point: a model that has misread the rule shows you on the first turn.
  2. Submit the transcript. Save the whole conversation and hand it in with the assignment. It is not a formality and it is not only for catching people - it is the evidence behind the AI Review Log, and where the log says what you rejected, the transcript shows what you were actually offered.
