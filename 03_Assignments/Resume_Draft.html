Required length: 1 page

This is 1 page — and it must fill the page. Spilling onto a second page is a miss; so is handing in a half-empty one.

Page standard: 11 point serif, 1 inch margins, single spaced. Images, tables, captions and references do not count toward the page count; everything you wrote does.

Hitting a target exactly is not a formatting chore. It is the skill. Anyone can say something in unlimited space. Saying it in the space you were given — and no more — is what professional writing is, and it is what every employer, editor, reviewer and senior official you will ever write for requires of you.

Both directions are failures, and they fail differently. Running over means you did not decide what mattered. Running short means you did not have enough to say, or you stopped before finding it — and padding to reach the target is worse than either, because it wastes the reader’s attention on purpose.

On using AI here. AI will pad to a word count without complaint and it will do it well enough that you may not notice. Getting longer is not the problem you have. Ask it to cut instead: give it your draft and the target, tell it to remove the weakest fifteen percent, and then argue with what it chose. It will try to cut things that matter. Deciding what survives is the part that is actually yours.

The assignment

A resume targeted at one specific job advertisement that you choose and submit alongside it. The job advertisement is a required part of the submission, not a formality. A resume cannot be assessed in the abstract, because there is no such thing as a good resume in the abstract — there is only a resume that is well or badly aimed at a particular posting. Submitting a generic resume is the single most common mistake students make here, and it is the one employers filter out first.

Choosing the posting

  • Pick something you would actually apply to, now or on graduation.
  • It must be real and current. Bring the text of the posting, not just a link — postings disappear.
  • Something slightly beyond your current experience is a better choice than something safe. The gap is where the interesting work is.

What makes this work

  • Every line earns its place against that posting. If a bullet would sit unchanged on a resume for a different job, it is probably not doing much.
  • Accomplishments carry evidence — scale, tools, outcome. "Built a suitability model in ArcGIS Pro covering 254 Texas counties" beats "experience with GIS analysis."
  • The vocabulary of the posting appears where it is honestly true of you. Where it is not true, you do not fake it — you address the gap somewhere you can, or you accept it.
  • Readable at a glance. Consistent formatting.

What to submit

  1. Your resume, PDF.
  2. The job advertisement you targeted — full text.
  3. Your completed AI Review Log.
  4. The transcript of your AI conversation. The whole thing, saved and attached - not a summary of it and not the parts that went well. If you used more than one tool or more than one session, attach all of them.

What happens next

Feedback returns within one week. The Final keeps the same job advertisement and is graded on how substantively you responded to the feedback. Retargeting to a different posting would let you avoid the harder work of making this argument better.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Write your own first draft. This is a W assignment and the credit is for your writing, so the words you submit have to be yours. Use AI freely on everything around the draft - finding sources, checking a claim, arguing with what you have written - but not to produce the draft itself.
  2. Open the conversation by saying who you are and what the rule is. Paste this before anything else: "I am a student at Texas A&M University and I am bound by the Aggie Honor Code. This is a W course writing assignment and all the words I submit must be my own. For this assignment I may NOT use AI to generate words that I turn in. I am required to save and submit the transcript of this conversation with my assignment. Restate those rules back to me in your own words before we start." Asking it to restate rather than to agree is the point: a model that has misread the rule shows you on the first turn.
  3. Submit the transcript. Save the whole conversation and hand it in with the assignment. It is not a formality and it is not only for catching people - it is the evidence behind the AI Review Log, and where the log says what you rejected, the transcript shows what you were actually offered.
  4. Paste the job advertisement into the tool along with your resume. Every persona on this assignment needs it. A reviewer that cannot see the posting can only give you generic advice, which is the kind you already have.
  5. Run Resume Screening Software first. There is no point polishing prose for a human reader if the automated screen would filter you out before one sees it.
  6. Then run the remaining personas: the six-second recruiter scan, the hiring manager, and the evidence auditor.
  7. Revise in response to the critiques you find persuasive.
  8. Deliberately identify at least two critiques you reject, and be able to explain why the reviewer was wrong. AI is confidently wrong about resumes fairly often — it likes buzzwords and it will pad.
  9. Complete the AI Review Log and attach it.

Peer review required — AI required, used critically

You will review 1 classmate submission(s) through Canvas.

Reviews open: Thursday 8 October, at the deadline  ·  Reviews due: Monday 12 October, 11:59 PM

Focus your review on:

  • Read the job advertisement first, then the resume. Does the resume answer the posting?
  • Which bullets would survive on a resume for a completely different job? Those are the weak ones.
  • Is there evidence — numbers, tools, scale, outcome — or only claims?
  • Does it read as a real person, or as language generated to sound professional?

AI is required for this review — in this order:

  1. Read it yourself first. No AI. Write down two or three things you think are strong and two or three you think are weak. Do this before you open any AI tool — and paste that list at the top of your review, unchanged. It is the only proof that you formed a view of your own, and it is the first thing read.
  2. Now run the AI reviewer personas on the draft, using the peer-review prompts in the AI Prompt Library.
  3. Compare. What did AI catch that you missed? What did you catch that AI missed? Where is AI simply wrong about this draft?
  4. Write the review in your own voice, marking which points came from your own reading, which from AI, and where you overruled AI.

Why that order. If you read the AI critique first you will anchor to it, and you will lose the ability to judge whether it is any good. Forming your own view first is what makes you a reviewer rather than a relay.

How this is graded. Not on how much you wrote. On what you added beyond what the AI already said, and on where you correctly judged the AI to be wrong. A review that is a lightly edited AI output earns very little.

Professional note: you are putting a classmate’s unpublished work into a tool. Use TAMU AI Chat or Copilot, which are university-protected, and treat their draft the way you would want yours treated.
