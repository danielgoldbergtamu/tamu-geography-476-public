Required time: 1 minute

This is 1 minute. Not seven, not eleven. You will be stopped at the limit, and running short is just as much a miss as running over.

Hitting a target exactly is not a formatting chore. It is the skill. Anyone can say something in unlimited space. Saying it in the space you were given — and no more — is what professional writing is, and it is what every employer, editor, reviewer and senior official you will ever write for requires of you.

Both directions are failures, and they fail differently. Running over means you did not decide what mattered. Running short means you did not have enough to say, or you stopped before finding it — and padding to reach the target is worse than either, because it wastes the reader’s attention on purpose.

On using AI here. AI will pad to a word count without complaint and it will do it well enough that you may not notice. Getting longer is not the problem you have. Ask it to cut instead: give it your draft and the target, tell it to remove the weakest fifteen percent, and then argue with what it chose. It will try to cut things that matter. Deciding what survives is the part that is actually yours.

The assignment

is TxGIS Day. Our class presents in its own session. If you chose the one-minute format, you deliver one message in sixty seconds to a public audience.

Why sixty seconds is hard

A minute does not let you explain your method, justify your data, or build to a conclusion. It lets you say one thing. Choosing which one thing — and cutting everything else — is the skill being assessed. This is closer to how findings actually get communicated to busy decision-makers than any other format in this course.

What makes this work

  • One message, stated in the first ten seconds.
  • One visual, readable from the back of the room.
  • You finish inside sixty seconds. Practice with a clock; you will run over the first several times.

Preparation

You rehearse this in under hostile questioning, and submit the final file by 9:00 AM on the day.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Open the conversation by saying who you are and what the rule is. Paste this before anything else: "I am a student at Texas A&M University and I am bound by the Aggie Honor Code. This is not a writing assignment for the W designation, so no rule about whose words these are applies. For this assignment I MAY use AI to generate words that I turn in. I am required to save and submit the transcript of this conversation with my assignment. Restate those rules back to me in your own words before we start." Asking it to restate rather than to agree is the point: a model that has misread the rule shows you on the first turn.
  2. Submit the transcript. Save the whole conversation and hand it in with the assignment. It is not a formality and it is not only for catching people - it is the evidence behind the AI Review Log, and where the log says what you rejected, the transcript shows what you were actually offered.

Top critiquesThe three strongest critiques each persona raised.
Changes madeWhat you actually changed in response, and where.

The “critiques rejected” field carries the most weight. Accepting every suggestion an AI makes is not review — it is transcription. Show us where you overruled it, and why you were right to.
