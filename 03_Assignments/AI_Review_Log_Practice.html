The assignment

Submit the practice AI Review Log you filled in during the first studio. This carries no marks and cannot affect your grade in any way. It is here for two reasons, and both of them are for you.

Why it exists

So the format is familiar before it counts. The first Review Log that carries marks is several weeks away. Meeting the five fields for the first time under deadline, on work you care about, is a worse introduction than meeting them now on an essay from another class. So you can look back at it. Keep this. In December you will write a Review Log for your final report, and the most useful thing you can do at that point is open this one and read what you wrote in week one. Most students find the difference obvious and are surprised by it — the week-one log tends to accept whatever the tool said, and the December log argues with it.

What to submit

The completed practice log, as text or as a file. All five fields: was wrong. Field 5 will probably be the hardest, and it is supposed to be. If you cannot find anything to reject, say so honestly — that is a real answer in week one, and it is exactly the thing that changes.

  1. Tools used — which tool, which model.
  2. Personas run — which reviewer roles, and the actual prompt you gave each.
  3. Top critiques — the strongest points each persona raised.
  4. Changes made — what you would change in response, and where.
  5. Critiques rejected — what you would not act on, and why the reviewer
  6. The transcript of your AI conversation. The whole thing, saved and attached - not a summary of it and not the parts that went well. If you used more than one tool or more than one session, attach all of them.

How it is assessed

It is not. There is no grade attached and there is nothing to lose. For transparency: completed logs are read against the same AI Competency scale used later in the course, for the instructor's own record of how the class develops over a semester. That reading has no effect on your marks, and any use of this material beyond this course is anonymized and covered by the course's stated research approach.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Open the conversation by saying who you are and what the rule is. Paste this before anything else: "I am a student at Texas A&M University and I am bound by the Aggie Honor Code. This is not a writing assignment for the W designation, so no rule about whose words these are applies. For this assignment I MAY use AI to generate words that I turn in. I am required to save and submit the transcript of this conversation with my assignment. Restate those rules back to me in your own words before we start." Asking it to restate rather than to agree is the point: a model that has misread the rule shows you on the first turn.
  2. Submit the transcript. Save the whole conversation and hand it in with the assignment. It is not a formality and it is not only for catching people - it is the evidence behind the AI Review Log, and where the log says what you rejected, the transcript shows what you were actually offered.

Required submission: AI Review Log

Attach a completed AI Review Log to your submission. Same five fields every time.

Required fields of the AI Review Log
Tool(s) usedWhich AI tool(s), e.g. TAMU AI Chat (Claude Sonnet), M365 Copilot.
Personas runWhich reviewer personas you used, and the prompt you gave each.
Top critiquesThe three strongest critiques each persona raised.
Changes madeWhat you actually changed in response, and where.
Critiques rejectedWhat you chose not to act on, and why.

The “critiques rejected” field carries the most weight. Accepting every suggestion an AI makes is not review — it is transcription. Show us where you overruled it, and why you were right to.

Required submission: your AI transcript

Attach the whole conversation alongside the AI Review Log. The log is what you claim about your use of AI; the transcript is the evidence for the claim, and it is the half that cannot be reconstructed afterward. Where the log says which critiques you rejected, the transcript shows what you were actually offered.
