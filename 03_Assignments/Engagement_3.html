The assignment

What you hand in is your review of your partner's presentation. Text entry or a file. Nothing else. The due date is on this assignment in Canvas. This is the third of nine engagement checks, and it is the one where the session itself produces the artifact. In studio you picked a partner, scored their sixty-second pitch against the four Project Pitch criteria, and gave them one thing that worked and one thing to change. That page is the submission.

Photograph your page before you hand it over

You give the page to your partner at the end of class, because it is their feedback and they need it. So photograph it first. A phone photograph is enough and it is the whole upload — you do not have to type it up, redraw it, or make it presentable. If you would rather type it, type it. If you handed the page over and did not photograph it, write the same thing again from memory and say that is what you did.

What you turn in

Your partner's four scores, with a reason for each, plus the two sentences you gave them.

This is the complete list. If it is not here it is not required, and if it is here it is not optional. Everything goes in one submission.

  1. Who you reviewed, and their pitch number.
  2. Four scores, 0 to 4, one per criterion: Component Definition, Connection to the Client Question, AI Competency, Length Discipline.
  3. A reason for each score. What in their sixty seconds earned it. A number on its own is not feedback and does not count here.
  4. One thing that worked, and one thing to change and how.
  5. One line on the room: a component that overlaps yours, or a gap nobody claimed.
  6. If you used AI on this, the transcript of that conversation. AI is not required here. If you did use it, the whole conversation comes in with the work - not a summary of it, and not only the parts that went well.

Why it is graded this way

Engagement is not attendance. Attendance means you were in the room; engagement is whether the work of the session got done by you — and on this Thursday the work of the session was judging somebody else's claim against a stated standard and telling them something they can act on. Rating your partner is the easy half. The reason is the assignment. A four with no reason behind it teaches the presenter nothing and shows me nothing about your judgment. The same skill is what every AI Review Log asks for all term: not whether you liked the output, but what in it earned the verdict you gave. This is also the first time peer review happens in this course. The protocol you owe on graded work — your own reading first, AI second — is taught in S04. Today is the human half of it, and this is where it is recorded.

How this is assessed

Against the Engagement rubric, applied to what you hand in. Four scores with four reasons, the two sentences, and the line on the room earns full points. Scores with no reasons is half an assignment. You are not graded on whether your scores match mine — you are graded on whether you can say what earned them.

If you use AI on this, two rules apply

This is not one of the assignments that requires AI, and nothing here obliges you to open a tool. But the two rules below hold everywhere in this course, including here: if you use AI on something you hand in, you announce yourself first and you hand in the transcript with it.

  1. Open the conversation by saying who you are and what the rule is. Paste this before anything else: "I am a student at Texas A&M University and I am bound by the Aggie Honor Code. This is not a writing assignment for the W designation, so no rule about whose words these are applies. For this assignment I MAY use AI to generate words that I turn in. I am required to save and submit the transcript of this conversation with my assignment. Restate those rules back to me in your own words before we start." Asking it to restate rather than to agree is the point: a model that has misread the rule shows you on the first turn.
  2. Submit the transcript. Save the whole conversation and hand it in with the assignment. It is not a formality and it is not only for catching people - it is the evidence behind the AI Review Log, and where the log says what you rejected, the transcript shows what you were actually offered.
