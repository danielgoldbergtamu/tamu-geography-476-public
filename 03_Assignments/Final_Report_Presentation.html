Required time: 12 minutes

This is 12 minutes. Not seven, not eleven. You will be stopped at the limit, and running short is just as much a miss as running over.

Hitting a target exactly is not a formatting chore. It is the skill. Anyone can say something in unlimited space. Saying it in the space you were given — and no more — is what professional writing is, and it is what every employer, editor, reviewer and senior official you will ever write for requires of you.

Both directions are failures, and they fail differently. Running over means you did not decide what mattered. Running short means you did not have enough to say, or you stopped before finding it — and padding to reach the target is worse than either, because it wastes the reader’s attention on purpose.

On using AI here. AI will pad to a word count without complaint and it will do it well enough that you may not notice. Getting longer is not the problem you have. Ask it to cut instead: give it your draft and the target, tell it to remove the weakest fifteen percent, and then argue with what it chose. It will try to cut things that matter. Deciding what survives is the part that is actually yours.

The assignment

Present your component and its findings. Three minutes maximum, delivered in class on .

Format: lead with the answer

Finding first. A senior official who heard only your first minute should come away with the answer and your confidence in it.

What makes this work

  • The finding leads, with confidence stated and justified.
  • Information gaps are presented as findings, not apologies. Given that this cohort works with rough open-source data ahead of the NGA agreement, what you could not determine is genuinely useful to the client and to the students who follow you.
  • You can defend your own component under questioning, unaided.
  • "I do not know — here is what would tell us" is a strong answer. Practice it.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Write your own first draft. This is a W course and the credit is for your writing, so the words you submit have to be yours. Use AI freely on everything around the draft — finding sources, checking a claim, arguing with what you have written — but not to produce the draft itself. The full list of what AI may and may not do is on the AI Usage in this Class page, under "What AI may and may not do, item by item".
  2. Run your draft past all the reviewer personas listed for this assignment, using the prompts in the AI Prompt Library.
  3. Revise in response to the critiques you find persuasive.
  4. Deliberately identify at least two critiques you reject, and be able to explain why the reviewer was wrong.
  5. Complete the AI Review Log and attach it.
