The assignment

Half a page. Text entry or a file. The due date is on this assignment in Canvas. Engagement is the weekly check that the work of the session actually got done by you. This is the first one, and it covers the first studio — getting set up with AI.

What to hand in

Three things, and no more. Three sentences is often enough. This is not a reflection and it is not an essay. It is the evidence that the session's work got done, written down so it can be marked.

  1. What you got working. Which of the tools you reached — TAMU AI Chat, Copilot — and on which machine. If something did not work, say what and where it stopped; that is more useful to me than a clean answer.
  2. What you ran it on. The piece of your own writing you pointed a reviewer persona at, and one thing it told you that you did not accept — with the reason you did not accept it.
  3. What you would do differently next time you ask one of these for something. One sentence.
  4. If you used AI on this, the transcript of that conversation. AI is not required here. If you did use it, the whole conversation comes in with the work - not a summary of it, and not only the parts that went well.

Why it is graded this way

Engagement used to have nothing to upload. That made it impossible for you to see how it was being marked and impossible for me to apply the rubric to anything, which is a fair thing to have been confused about. It now takes a submission, and this page is what that submission is. Later engagements ask for different things. Once teams are formed and each of you has a Project Manager, the account covers what you produced for the team, the review you gave a teammate, and what you reported upward. None of that exists yet in week one, so this one asks only about the work you actually did in the room.

How this is assessed

Against the Engagement rubric, adapted to what week one can show: whether you were present and working, whether you got the tools running, and whether you can say what the tool got wrong and why you rejected it. The last of those is the one this course cares about most, and it is the same judgment every AI Review Log asks for all term.

If you use AI on this, two rules apply

This is not one of the assignments that requires AI, and nothing here obliges you to open a tool. But the two rules below hold everywhere in this course, including here: if you use AI on something you hand in, you announce yourself first and you hand in the transcript with it.

  1. Open the conversation by saying who you are and what the rule is. Paste this before anything else: "I am a student at Texas A&M University and I am bound by the Aggie Honor Code. This is not a writing assignment for the W designation, so no rule about whose words these are applies. For this assignment I MAY use AI to generate words that I turn in. I am required to save and submit the transcript of this conversation with my assignment. Restate those rules back to me in your own words before we start." Asking it to restate rather than to agree is the point: a model that has misread the rule shows you on the first turn.
  2. Submit the transcript. Save the whole conversation and hand it in with the assignment. It is not a formality and it is not only for catching people - it is the evidence behind the AI Review Log, and where the log says what you rejected, the transcript shows what you were actually offered.
