# GEOG 476 · GIS Practicum — the course pages

**Texas A&M University, Department of Geography.** These are the Canvas pages of a
writing-intensive GIS capstone rebuilt so that **AI use is required rather than
permitted**, and so that what is graded is the student's judgment about AI output
rather than whether they used it.

They are published so that colleagues at other institutions can take what is useful.
Take a page, take an assignment, take one rubric criterion — none of it depends on the
rest.

**Contact:** Daniel W. Goldberg, daniel.goldberg@tamu.edu

---

## What this is

**114 pages**, in four folders: the syllabus, 75 reference and session pages, 37 assignments and 1 rubric page.

Every page is standalone HTML with its styling inline. There is no build step, no
JavaScript, and no images — open any file in a browser, or paste its source into a
Canvas page and it arrives intact.

## Browse it, or take a copy

**Browse:** https://danielgoldbergtamu.github.io/tamu-geography-476-public/ — the
pages link to each other, so you can read the course the way a student meets it.

**Take a copy:** the green **Code** button above → **Download ZIP**, or

```
git clone https://github.com/danielgoldbergtamu/tamu-geography-476-public.git
```

## What you will have to change

- **The syllabus** is Texas A&M's, and carries this university's required policy
  language. Replace it with your own.
- **The two client projects** are real engagements with real organizations. Yours will
  be different; the structure of the project pages is the transferable part.
- **Dates and week numbers** are Fall 2026.
- **The AI tools named** are the ones Texas A&M contracts and approves. Name yours.

## What is not here, and why

**The processing that produces these pages is not published.** The builders, the
content registers, the gates, the department report and the census paper stay in the
private repository. What is here is the output a student sees.

**Two PDFs the course teaches from are absent.** They were written by John
O'Neil, Jim Lawler and Mike Mears, and were shared with this class by their
authors. The permission granted was to share them with a class, not to
republish them, so the files are excluded and every page that linked to them
now says so where the link was. The pages still tell you what the
documents argue and why they are assigned.

**Two embedded slide decks are absent** for the same practical reason: they
are hosted on a university Google Drive that is not public.

**Two pages are held back pending client review.** They carry quotations from
a client meeting, and the agreement in that meeting was that material is sent
for expert approval before it is published. They are live for the class in
Canvas; they are not published here until the client's communications office
has read them.
