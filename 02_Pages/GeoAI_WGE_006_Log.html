1. Why the cattle constraint makes this tractable

A person crossing on foot leaves an enormous feasible set: thirty miles overnight through brush on one bottle of water. A herd does not. The constraints that bind, roughly in order:

  • Water, daily. Cattle drink 10 to 20 gallons a day in South Texas heat. The route has to touch water every day, which in practice means the river and then stock tanks.
  • Drive rate. Eight to fifteen miles a day without stripping condition. The destination therefore sits within about one night's drive of the river — which is exactly the ground between the river and the hundred-mile line drawn in plan_sq04b.
  • A fordable place. Cattle swim but refuse fast or deep water, and need gentle, firm banks on BOTH sides. This is why the crossing set is small, and why it moves with river stage.
  • Forage on the route. They graze as they go.
  • Brush, and it is not monotonic. Dense thornscrub hides a herd and is also impassable to drive one through. The optimum is broken brush.
  • Fence. Any net-wire fence stops a herd.
  • Terrain. Canyon country above Del Rio rules out long stretches outright.
  • A load-out. Vehicle access within reach on the US side.

The framing this gets presented under is surveillance prioritisation, not a route guide. It is what the USDA tick riders already do from institutional knowledge, made explicit and therefore testable. That sentence goes on the slide, not in the speaker notes.

3. The pulls that worked

https://copernicus-dem-90m.s3.amazonaws.com/... — navigated the browser straight to each of twelve tile URLs. Each landed in _Inbox within about three seconds. GLO-30 was available at the same address pattern and was deliberately NOT used: 90 m is already finer than the 231 m analysis grid, and 30 m would have been 480 MB to gain nothing.

https://esa-worldcover.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/v200/2021/map/ESA_WorldCover_10m_2021_v200_N27W102_Map.tif — one 3-degree tile, 89 MB, about thirty seconds. It covers the study box exactly, which is why the box was set to -101.6..-99.1, 27.4..29.9 rather than being drawn first and tiled to fit. The tile boundary chose the frame.

WorldCover was chosen over NLCD for one reason: NLCD stops at the river. A model of crossing that cannot see the far bank is not a model of crossing.

4. The analysis grid, and why it is 25 cells

The grid is 0.00208333 degrees, which is EXACTLY 25 ESA WorldCover cells. Land cover is categorical, and resampling a categorical raster with anything other than counting invents classes that were never there. On a whole-number block the aggregation is a count: the fraction of each cell in each class, which is more informative than a majority class anyway. The DEM is continuous and is reprojected onto the same grid by averaging, which is right for a field and wrong for a category.

The land cover tile is 36,000 x 36,000 uint8 — 1.3 GB read whole. It is read in bands of 2,500 source rows, 100 output rows at a time.

5. The first pass, and why it failed usefully

grid 1200 x 1200 at 0.002083 deg
slope done: median 0.92 deg, 99th 17.16 deg
water: 19611 wet cells, median distance 2.5 km
suitability: min 0.112  median 0.771  90th 0.842  max 1.000

Distribution of the 1,420,389 scored cells:

p1 0.372   p10 0.573   p25 0.667   p50 0.771   p75 0.830   p90 0.842   p99 0.951
63.5 percent of all ground scores between 0.70 and 0.85

The surface is nearly flat, and that is the result. Three reasons, each of which is a real property of this landscape rather than a bug:

  1. Slope never binds. Median 0.92 degrees; the slope term averages 0.954 of a possible 1.0. This is not canyon country. It would be above Del Rio, and the frame does not reach that far.
  2. Water never binds either, and this was not expected. 19,611 water cells in the box, median distance to water 2.5 km. South Texas ranchland is full of stock tanks. The constraint that husbandry says is the sharpest is close to satisfied everywhere in the frame.
  3. Land cover barely varies. 73.9 percent of cells are majority SHRUBLAND (WorldCover class 20). One class covers three-quarters of the landscape, so a per-class score has almost nothing to separate.

Teaching point, and it is the reason to keep a first pass rather than delete it: the three constraints that were easiest to get are the three that do not discriminate here. A model that returns "almost everywhere is fine" has not failed to find an answer — it has found that the binding constraints are elsewhere. The full model has to reach for fence, riverbank and destination, and now there is a measured reason to say so rather than an assertion.

6. The full model: score the river, not the ground

If slope, water and cover are satisfied nearly everywhere, then what decides a crossing is not the ground in general — it is the RIVER and the WAY OUT. So the second model does not score cells. It samples the international boundary every kilometre — 411 points across 410.9 km of river in frame — and scores each on six things:

6. The full model: score the river, not the ground
termhow it is obtained
bank relief5th-to-95th percentile elevation range within 500 m of each bank, on the 90 m DEM
channelpermanent-water fraction on a 400 m disc
approach covermean first-pass suitability within 1.5 km either side
built-upWorldCover class 50 fraction within 2 km
barriermetres of MAPPED barrier within 1 km
accessstraight-line distance from the US bank to the nearest road a stock trailer can reach

Weights live in crossing_model.yaml: bank 0.30, access 0.25, cover 0.20, built 0.10, channel 0.10, barrier 0.05. Barrier is weighted LAST despite being conceptually decisive, and the reason is written next to the number.

6.1 The two new pulls

TIGER 2023 roads, ten counties, by navigating straight to https://www2.census.gov/geo/tiger/TIGER2023/ROADS/tl_2023_<fips>_roads.zip — 58,540 segments, filtered to MTFCC S1100, S1200, S1400, S1500 and S1740, the classes a stock trailer can actually use.

OpenStreetMap barriers, and this one needed a different technique. Navigating to an Overpass URL does not work — Overpass returns application/json, which Chrome renders rather than downloads. But Overpass sends Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *, so a fetch from any page succeeds. So: count first (out count → 1,282 ways), then fetch with geometry, convert to GeoJSON in the page, and trigger a blob download. 399 KB, one round trip.

6.2 The result, and the one number that matters most

score   min 0.540   p25 0.726   p50 0.815   p75 0.895   max 0.982

It discriminates, where the first pass did not.

Then the held-out test. The three legal ports of entry were never an input to the model. It does not know they exist. Asked where it ranks the river kilometre nearest each one:

Laredo       rank 406 of 411   bottom  1%   score 0.608   barrier 6,815 m
Eagle Pass   rank 396 of 411   bottom  4%   score 0.641   barrier 1,006 m
Del Rio      rank 311 of 411   bottom 24%   score 0.720   barrier 1,417 m

A model built only from terrain, land cover, mapped barrier and road access independently puts the places where cattle legally cross among the worst places to move a herd across unofficially. That is the right answer and nothing told it.

6.3 The check that stops it being a trick

The obvious objection is that the score is just "distance from town", and the ports are in towns. It is not:

corr(score, distance to nearest port) = -0.036
corr(score, bank relief)              = -0.61
  ... and among the 257 river km MORE than 25 km from any port:
corr(score, bank relief)              = -0.75

Essentially zero against proximity; strong against the riverbank. The terrain is doing the work. Running that check was not optional — a validation you have not tried to break is a compliment you paid yourself.

6.4 What the model says, and what it must not be read as saying

Five clusters in the top decile; the two largest are 13 km around 28.93 N, 100.62 W (between Eagle Pass and Del Rio) and 11 km around 28.19 N, 100.15 W (south of Eagle Pass toward the Webb County line).

The frame, and it is on the face of the map: surveillance prioritisation, not a route guide. This is what the USDA tick riders already do from institutional knowledge, made explicit so that it can be tested and argued with.

And the honest weakness, also on the face of the map: OSM carries about 71 km of mapped barrier within 2 km of 411 km of river, nearly all of it inside the three towns. A low barrier score means nobody has mapped a fence, not that no fence exists. Ranch fencing is the real constraint on the ground and it is essentially unmapped. That is a named gap and a concrete ask — TAHC and USDA hold what would close it.

7. Two accessibility defects found by testing, not by a checker

One. The first render put the bottom decile in a pale tone. Under the simulated projector wash (out = pixel * 0.52 + 112) those segments vanished entirely — and a vanished class does not read as "low rank", it reads as no river there at all. A class that disappears is a class the reader silently misreads. Fixed by drawing the WHOLE river first in a tone dark enough to survive the wash, and letting the top and middle classes ride on top of it. Every checker passed both before and after.

Two. The first evidence slide for SQ4 printed its finding straight through the "DATA WE USED" heading, and the fix for that then printed the third source line off the bottom edge. Both came from the same mistake: sizing the figure first and hoping the words fit. evidence_slide now measures both text blocks and gives the figure what is left.

9. Paying the debt the work created

Three gates were red after the maps were built, and all three were about where things LIVE rather than whether they were right.

9.1 A ratchet caught 3,223 characters of captions in a generator

data_in_code_check refuses new content in tools/. figs_plans.py was carrying 36 literals — every caption, subtitle and on-map label for nine specimen figures. They were lifted into content/projects/plan_figure_text.yaml, keyed by the function that draws them, and the generator now reads them.

The lift was done by a script, and the script broke the file the first time. ast reports col_offset and end_col_offset as byte offsets into the UTF-8 line, not character indices. This file is full of em dashes — three bytes, one character — so slicing each line as a str cut in the wrong place and ate the closing parentheses off a legend(...) call. The symptom was a SyntaxError two lines below the real damage. Fixed by doing every slice on bytes.

Then the lift was verified rather than assumed. A second script re-parsed the rewritten file, resolved every T[fn][key] lookup through the register, and compared the result to the original literals, in order, per function:

lossless: True -- 36 literals across 12 functions

That check is the only reason to trust an automated edit to a file nobody is going to read line by line.

9.2 A duplicate key that YAML had silently been discarding

yaml_key_check found figure: defined twice inside two plan blocks. YAML keeps the last and drops the first without a word, so the earlier one had already been deleted everywhere the file was read. Both were removed; the register now passes across 102 files.

9.3 205 MB in the wrong tree, and the rule that already said so

layout_check went from 135 problems to 32. Most of the fix was one honest decision. The content rule says, in as many words, that allowing .geojson "is not a license to put a raster archive in a git repository" — and the model needs 205 MB of Copernicus terrain, ESA WorldCover and TIGER roads.

So the split is by size and readability, not by file type:

  • _Data/screwworm_crossing/, declared and git-ignored, holds the terrain, the land cover, the road shapefiles and the 19.8 MB surface.
  • content/projects/ keeps what a person can read and diff: the 411 scored river kilometres, the 149 crossing volumes, the 398 Mexican cases, the extracted geometry and the registers.
  • content/projects/sources/, newly declared, holds the audit trail — the regulation text as it was read, and the 969 KB APHIS map graphic that 60 transcribed numbers depend on. A transcription whose source has been replaced upstream is a transcription nobody can check.

R91 is satisfied by a build input that can be re-obtained and verified, not by one that is committed. tools/fetch_border_movement.py names the exact request for every file and fails the build if a checksum has drifted.
