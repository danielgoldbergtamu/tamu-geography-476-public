This page explains the two week-one quizzes. It is not a submission and carries no points; open the quizzes from this week's module.

What it is

Two short Canvas quizzes, taken in that order and in one sitting:

  1. GIS + AI Knowledge Check — twenty questions, about twenty minutes.
  2. AI Practice Inventory — how you actually work, how confident you feel, and one question asking you to predict your own score.

Do them in class on Tuesday if there is time. Otherwise do them at home before the Thursday studio.

What it counts for

Nothing, beyond participation. There are no points attached to either quiz and neither can affect your grade in any other way. Completing them counts toward participation; the answers do not.

Why it exists

To find out where to pitch this course. This is the same thing a mathematics department does with a placement check in the first week: nobody actually knows what a given room of students already knows about working with these tools, and every syllabus in the country is currently guessing. This one stops guessing.

To give you your own starting point. Canvas marks the Knowledge Check immediately and explains every answer, including the ones you got right. You get a profile by topic — directing the tool, evaluation and verification, sources and fabrication, GIS-specific failure modes, disclosure and professional use, and AI applied to GIS work itself. Most people are surprised by at least one of them.

To measure what changes. You take the same check again in the final week. The difference is yours to look at, and in aggregate it is how this course finds out whether requiring AI on the project work actually builds the judgment it is meant to build.

How to answer it

Do not look anything up, and do not answer strategically. A diagnostic that flatters you is useless to both of us — if the results say this class already understands verification, I will teach less of it, and you will get a term aimed at a class that does not exist.

Some of the questions are genuinely hard, and several cover things this course has not taught you yet. That is deliberate. Getting them wrong in week one is the expected outcome and costs you nothing.

There is a question at the end asking how many you think you got right. Answer it honestly and before you look at your score — knowing how much you know is a separate skill from knowing it, and it is one of the things worth watching over a semester.

What to submit

Nothing beyond completing both quizzes in Canvas. The quizzes themselves are what you open and what records that you did it — this page only explains them.

What happens to your answers

You get your own results. I use the aggregate — never individual results — to decide what to teach and in how much depth. Aggregate results may appear in published work about how this course was designed, de-identified before anyone analyzes them. No individual result is ever shown to anyone but you.
