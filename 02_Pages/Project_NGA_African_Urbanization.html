The question you are being asked

At what rates are major African cities urbanizing, spreading and sprawling; what does that imply for infrastructure strain and for foreign influence in those countries; and can the method that measures it be applied anywhere else?

The supporting questions

  1. Where is the edge of this city, under a rule you can state and defend?
  2. How has that edge moved, decade by decade, in the historic imagery record?
  3. What is the rate of areal expansion, and is it accelerating?
  4. Is growth infill, edge expansion or leapfrog — and in what proportion?
  5. What infrastructure appeared alongside the growth, and when?
  6. Which of that infrastructure is externally financed, and by whom?
  7. Which cities are outliers against their peers, and what explains it?
  8. What do the next ten years look like, and how wrong could that be?
  9. Does the method transfer to a city nobody in this room has looked at?

Why it matters on the ground

Nigeria is the most populous country in Africa and among the fastest-urbanizing places on earth. Settlements expand, roads appear, farmland converts — and the formal record of who owns what lags far behind what is physically on the ground.

That gap, between the landscape as it exists and the landscape as it is recorded, is the thing your work measures. It has consequences well beyond the client’s question: for planning, for food security, for dispute resolution, for humanitarian response, and for who can safely invest in what.

Keep that in view alongside the analytical framing below. NGA is asking about influence and infrastructure strain; the people living on the expanding edge of these cities are asking whether anyone knows they are there.

Why NGA wants it

Keep this in view. It is the difference between measuring something and answering someone’s question.

  • Direct analytic effort toward specific African cities and countries
  • Monitor infrastructure developments and opportunities for foreign influence
  • Understand which countries or regions may be at risk of resource and infrastructure strain
  • Highlight which African nations may require outside assistance for infrastructure development — the brief names Russia, China, the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Turkey — leading to expanded economic influence across the continent

Background as NGA states it: Africa is in an unprecedented urban population boom, growing at the fastest rate in the world. Economic growth has not kept pace, with implications for foreign direct investment, trade and infrastructure. Africa remains heavily reliant on China, its largest trading partner.

The methodology is the deliverable too

From the brief: “The specific methodology proposed in this brief has not been attempted on an NGA product and, if successful, could be applied to other cities around the world for anticipatory analysis.”

Read that again. If your method works, it may be used far beyond Africa. Document it as though someone else will have to run it — because they might.

What a strong component looks like

  • Another team could apply your protocol to a different city and get a comparable number.
  • Your growth rate is traceable to specific imagery, on specific dates, with stated processing.
  • You can say which part of your result is measurement and which part is model.
  • Your forecast has been tested against history it did not see.
  • Claims about who financed what are sourced to reporting, never inferred from pixels alone.

Why take this project

Measuring how fast a city grows is a solid remote sensing problem. Working out who is shaping that growth, and to whose benefit, is a geopolitical one. This project is both, and the second half is the part NGA actually cares about.

You would be doing something genuinely new

NGA's own brief says the proposed methodology has not been attempted on an NGA product, and that if it works it could be applied to other cities around the world for anticipatory analysis. That is an extraordinary sentence to find in an undergraduate project. There is no answer key, nobody has done this and graded it before, and a method that works does not stop at Africa.

Skills that compound

Multi-decade historic imagery time series, defensible boundary delineation (far harder than it sounds — where exactly is the edge of Lagos?), distinguishing infill from edge expansion from leapfrog development, and forecast modeling with honest uncertainty. Then the harder half: identifying Chinese-financed Belt and Road infrastructure from imagery and open reporting, and establishing what can and cannot be shown about land ownership on an urban fringe.

You will be working on the actual question

Not a proxy for it. How foreign influence is exercised through development finance, where it is physically visible on the ground, and which cities are heading for infrastructure strain — that is what the brief names Russia, China, the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia and Turkey for. Africa's urban population is growing faster than anywhere on earth and its economies are not keeping pace. The consequences of that are being decided now.

Name another graduate who can say this

“I can identify Belt and Road infrastructure signatures in satellite imagery and tell you what they imply about influence in a region.” There are professional analysts who cannot say that. It is the kind of thing that makes an interviewer stop reading the resume and start asking questions — which is the entire point of choosing a hard project.

Your work does not stop in December

The data this semester is open source and rough, because the agreement that would give us better data could not be completed in time. It gets better in future semesters. What you build now — the protocol, the documented method, the honest account of where the evidence runs out — is what the next cohort and the INTA 670 graduate students inherit. Negative results count: we tried this, here is why it does not work, here is the evidence saves someone an entire semester.

The hard parts

  1. “Where does the city end?” has no natural answer. Administrative boundary, built-up footprint, commuting zone and lit area give four different cities and four different growth rates. Choosing is the analysis, and you must defend the choice.
  2. Spread and sprawl are not the same thing. Two cities can grow at identical rates with completely different morphologies. NGA asked for both.
  3. Historic imagery is not consistent. Sensors change, resolution changes, clouds intervene, archives have gaps. Comparability across decades must be engineered.
  4. Prediction invites overreach. The brief says attempt to predict. An honest forecast with stated uncertainty is worth more than a confident line on a chart.
  5. The foreign-influence framing is inferential. You cannot tell who financed a road from imagery alone. Say where the evidence stops.

AI on this project

AI is required on this project in two distinct ways, and both are graded. Neither is optional and neither is a formality.

What AI is required for on this project
On what you writeEvery deliverable carries an AI Review Log — the personas you ran, the actual prompts, what you changed, and what you rejected and why.
On the work itselfEvery project deliverable also carries an AI Method Log — where AI entered the technical work you own, what you asked, what it proposed, and how you verified it. Data discovery, projections, tool and parameter choice, scripting, debugging, quality checks. If you are the Project Manager, the same applies to the plan, the dependencies, the integration and the risk register.

The rule that governs both

You may not adopt a method you cannot defend independently of the tool that suggested it.

This client will act on what you hand them. A projection chosen because a model suggested it, a dataset cited because a model named it, or a statistic reported because a model computed it — none of those survive contact with someone who checks. Every source AI names must be opened before it is cited. It invents plausible dataset names and dead URLs, confidently, and that is the most common way this goes wrong.
