How the session runs

Establishes, suggests, is consistent with, does not show

These four are not degrees of politeness. They are four different claims about evidence, and choosing the right one is the interpretation work — most of what separates a professional finding from a student result is knowing which of these you have earned.

Establishes, suggests, is consistent with, does not show
The phraseWhat it claimsWhat it costs you to say it
EstablishesThe evidence here is sufficient. Somebody who disagrees has to attack the methodValidation, a stated uncertainty, and a result that survived somebody trying to break it
SuggestsThe evidence points this way and is not sufficient on its ownYou must say what would settle it — otherwise this is a hedge rather than a claim
Is consistent withThe evidence does not contradict this. It is also consistent with other explanationsYou have to name at least one of those other explanations, or the phrase is doing dishonest work
Does not showStating the limit of your own result, on purposeNothing. It is the cheapest sentence you will write and the one that protects everything else

Causal words are where this goes wrong. Drives, leads to, because of, causes, results in, explains — each asserts that changing one thing changes the other, which correlation cannot show on its own. Hunt every one in your own paragraph. For each, ask what you would have needed to show causation, and either produce it or downgrade the verb.

Run the Overclaim Detector, and judge which flags are right

The persona is in the AI Reviewer Persona Catalog. Paste your paragraph and it returns every sentence it believes claims more than the evidence supports, with a rewrite.

Judging the flags is the graded part, not collecting them. Expect it to be roughly right and specifically wrong, in three predictable ways.

  • It flags hedged sentences anyway. A sentence that already says suggests gets rewritten to say may possibly suggest. Reject that one and say why: the claim already matched the evidence, and vagueness is not accuracy.
  • It cannot see your validation. It only has the paragraph, so a claim you actually earned in S07 looks unearned to it. Reject it, and note that the fix is in your paragraph rather than in your result: if the reviewer cannot tell you validated it, neither can the client.
  • It misreads domain language. A technical term with a precise meaning gets read as a strong general claim. Reject it, and name the term.

Then act on the ones that are right, which there will be. Record the whole exchange in your AI Review Log — what it flagged, what you accepted, and what you rejected with the reason. Field 5, what you rejected and why, is the field that carries the marks.

Three flags rejected with good reasons is a better log than ten accepted without any.
