Before you arrive

  • Bring your figure and a one-sentence statement of your finding.

In studio — your component

  1. Lay out your contribution for three reading distances: three seconds, thirty seconds, three minutes.
  2. Cut text ruthlessly. A poster is not a paper on a wall.
  3. Make sure a reader can tell what you contributed.

In studio — the team

  1. Assemble the team poster. Argue about what gets the most space — that argument is the design.
  2. Check it at actual distance. Print small, stand back, or view at 25%.
  3. Confirm the main finding is legible from three feet.

Review each other's work, here, now

8 minutes, in class, and it replaces a Canvas peer review. Everything goes on the wall. Walk the room, then stand in front of the one that made you stop and say why. You are reviewing Project Poster, which you submit next Tuesday — this is the last time the room sees it before it is marked.

What to look for

  • From three feet and eight seconds: what did you register?
  • What competes for attention that should not?
  • Can you tell what this person contributed?

No written review is submitted for this. What is submitted is what you do next: the change you make because of what you heard, recorded in your AI Method Log or your next draft. Feedback you did not act on is feedback you did not get — and the person who told you it was wrong is sitting next to you, so ask them.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Run the Three-Feet Reviewer persona on today’s work. What survives poster-hall distance and eight seconds of attention.
  2. Read what it returns critically. Note anything it gets wrong about your component — that is the part worth recording.
  3. Record what you ran and what you did with it. This feeds the AI Review Log on whichever assignment this studio is building toward.
