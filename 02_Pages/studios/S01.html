Before you arrive

  • Read both project pages properly, not just the one you think you want.
  • Note which client problem you would actually be willing to spend fifteen weeks on.

In studio — your component

  1. For each client project, restate the client's question in your own words. If you cannot do it for a project, you do not understand that one yet — go back to its page.
  2. Pick the project you want and decompose it. List every distinguishable piece of work the problem contains. Aim for ten or more before you filter anything out.
  3. For each piece, note roughly what skills it needs and what data it would depend on.
  4. Identify three components you would be willing to own, ranked, and write one sentence on why you are suited to each.
  5. Note whether you want to be considered for Project Manager. Read How Projects Are Organized before you answer — it is not the easy option.
  6. Submit your ranked preferences. These are what I use to build teams.

In studio — the team

  1. No teams yet. Instead, compare decompositions with two or three people near you who chose the same project.
  2. Where your lists differ, work out why. Someone has usually spotted a piece of work the others missed.
  3. Flag to me any piece of the problem that appears on nobody's list — that is often the thing the project most needs.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Run the Skeptical Principal Investigator persona on today’s work. Attacks a framing before anyone commits fifteen weeks to it.
  2. Read what it returns critically. Note anything it gets wrong about your component — that is the part worth recording.
  3. Record what you ran and what you did with it. This feeds the AI Review Log on whichever assignment this studio is building toward.

Skeptical Principal Investigator

Leave with

  • A decomposition of your chosen project into ten or more candidate components
  • Your ranked top three components, with reasons
  • A stated position on the Project Manager role
  • Submitted preferences, which determine team formation
  • One line on any ethical friction you hit today — a license you were not sure covered your use, something your data reveals about people who never agreed to be in it, a claim you wanted to make and could not support. Most weeks there will be none, and that is a fine answer. These notes are what your Ethics Essay is built from.
