Before you arrive

  • Bring your results and any figures you have made so far.

In studio — your component

  1. Design the one figure that carries your component's argument. Not all your figures — the one.
  2. Interrogate every choice: projection, classification, color, normalization. Can you defend each?
  3. Check accessibility: does it work in grayscale, and for a color-blind reader?
  4. Write the caption. If the caption is hard to write, the figure is not finished.

In studio — the team

  1. Agree a shared visual language: color, typography, north arrows, scale bars, credits.
  2. Review each other's figures for consistency.

Review each other's work, here, now

11 minutes, in class, and it replaces a Canvas peer review. Swap with the person next to you. Read theirs, then talk — do not write a document. You are reviewing Project Draft 3, which was due on Tuesday.

What to look for

  • Is there real validation evidence, or is validation only mentioned?
  • Are edge cases and failures documented, or has the analysis been presented as smoother than it was?
  • Is any claim stronger than the evidence shown supports?

No written review is submitted for this. What is submitted is what you do next: the change you make because of what you heard, recorded in your AI Method Log or your next draft. Feedback you did not act on is feedback you did not get — and the person who told you it was wrong is sitting next to you, so ask them.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Run the Non-GIS Reviewer persona on today’s work. Can a smart person outside the field read this map correctly?
  2. Read what it returns critically. Note anything it gets wrong about your component — that is the part worth recording.
  3. Record what you ran and what you did with it. This feeds the AI Review Log on whichever assignment this studio is building toward.
