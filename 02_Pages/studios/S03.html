Before you arrive

  • Find your pitch number. The deck is grouped by project and then alphabetical by surname: Screwworm is 1 through 15, Urbanization is 16 through 27.
  • Know the three things you will say. Sixty seconds is roughly a hundred and fifty words.
  • Read the Project Pitch rubric before you arrive, not after. It is the four criteria you will be scored on today, and the four you will score somebody else on.
  • Re-read the candidate data sources on your project page. They are starting points, not answers.
  • Bring something to write on, and a phone. Engagement 3 is due Sep 22 and what you hand in is written in this room — your review of your partner's pitch, photographed before you give the page to them.
  • Listening is half of today's assignment.

In studio — your component

  1. Read the rubric on screen before you present. Four criteria, ten points: Component Definition (3.33, CLO4), Connection to the Client Question (2.22, CLO4), AI Competency (2.22, AI-C), Length Discipline (2.22, CLO5). These four are the words the feedback uses — both the instructor's and your partner's.
  2. Pick a partner before the first slide goes up. Somebody not on your project if the room allows it. Write down their name and their pitch number. You score their pitch on all four criteria; they score yours. That pairing is what gives you a job for the fifty-four minutes you are not presenting.
  3. Sixty seconds to present. Stand when your number appears on screen. Say three things: what your component produces, why the project needs it, and what you need from somebody else to build it.
  4. Sixty seconds of feedback, from the instructor, on the spot — one thing that worked and one thing to change.
  5. The clock is held. You will be stopped at sixty seconds, mid-sentence if necessary. Everything else is in the document you submitted.
  6. When your partner presents, score all four criteria and write one thing that worked and one thing to change.
  7. Listening is as much the assignment as presenting. While the rest present, write down a component that overlaps yours, a gap between two components that nobody has claimed, and anything you assumed the client wanted that somebody else heard differently.

In studio — the team

  1. Give your partner your four scores, with a reason for each. A number on its own is not feedback.
  2. Say the one thing that worked and the one thing to change, and how.
  3. Ask them the one question you still have about their component.
  4. Photograph your page. Then hand it to them. The page is their feedback and they need it — but the photograph is your submission, so take it first.
  5. That photograph is Engagement 3, due in Canvas Sep 22. Your review of your partner's presentation is the whole assignment; there is no separate write-up. If you handed the page over without photographing it, write the same thing again from memory and say that is what you did.
  6. Photograph your own notes too. The overlaps you wrote down are the only record of what this room worked out, and they are the input to conversations you owe before Project Draft 1.

How the session runs

The one lecture block today is fitness for use, and it comes last, after the presentations and the peer review: currency, resolution, completeness, license. Four questions to ask of any candidate dataset, in that order, and the first one you cannot answer is where your work is.

The thing worth taking away: there is no such thing as a good dataset, only a dataset good enough for the question being asked. The same layer is excellent for one component on this list and useless for the one beside it.

Review each other's work, here, now

5 minutes, in class, and it replaces a Canvas peer review. Swap with the person next to you. Read theirs, then talk — do not write a document. You are reviewing Project Pitch, which was due on Tuesday.

What to look for

  • Could you build this component from the description given?
  • Is the scope realistic for one person in fourteen weeks?
  • Does it overlap with your own component, or leave a gap between you?
  • Does it connect to something the client actually asked for?

No written review is submitted for this. What is submitted is what you do next: the change you make because of what you heard, recorded in your AI Method Log or your next draft. Feedback you did not act on is feedback you did not get — and the person who told you it was wrong is sitting next to you, so ask them.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Run the Data Quality Auditor persona on today’s work. Interrogates currency, resolution, completeness, license, provenance.
  2. Read what it returns critically. Note anything it gets wrong about your component — that is the part worth recording.
  3. Record what you ran and what you did with it. This feeds the AI Review Log on whichever assignment this studio is building toward.
