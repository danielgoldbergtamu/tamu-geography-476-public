Before you arrive

  • Re-read the candidate data sources on your project page. They are starting points, not answers.
  • Bring a laptop. Today is search and inspection work.

In studio — your component

  1. Build a source inventory for your component: every candidate dataset, its custodian, how to obtain it, its coverage, its currency.
  2. For each, note the standards it follows — format, CRS, metadata standard, any published specification.
  3. Find at least one source you did not know existed this morning. Ask the Data Librarian persona to name five sources for your component, then open every one of them. Some will not exist. Record which — this is the single most common way AI fails at GIS work, and today is the cheap place to learn it.
  4. Start your AI Method Log for this component, using the entry you just made: what you asked, what it named, and which of those sources survived being opened. This is practice — the first graded one is due with Project Draft 1 next week.
  5. Now get your most promising dataset. Actually download or access it. Open it. Look at it.
  6. Profile it: extent, resolution, completeness, CRS, units, missing values. Read its metadata — if there is none, that is a finding worth recording.
  7. Answer honestly: what would make this the wrong dataset for my question?
  8. Check the license. May you use it this way, and publish the result?

In studio — the team

  1. Build a shared data register so nobody duplicates a download or a license check.
  2. Agree team conventions now and write them down: CRS, units, file naming, folder structure.
  3. Compare profiles across components. Do resolutions and time periods actually align?
  4. Where they do not, decide now whether to resample, restrict scope, or find other data — and update the interface map if a contract has to change.

Review each other's work, here, now

10 minutes, in class, and it replaces a Canvas peer review. Two minutes each, around the table. You pitch, the next person asks the hardest question they have, you answer, move on. You are reviewing Project Pitch, which was due on Tuesday.

What to look for

  • Could you build this component from the description given?
  • Is the scope realistic for one person in fourteen weeks?
  • Does it overlap with your own component, or leave a gap between you?
  • Does it connect to something the client actually asked for?

No written review is submitted for this. What is submitted is what you do next: the change you make because of what you heard, recorded in your AI Method Log or your next draft. Feedback you did not act on is feedback you did not get — and the person who told you it was wrong is sitting next to you, so ask them.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Run the Data Quality Auditor persona on today’s work. Interrogates currency, resolution, completeness, license, provenance.
  2. Read what it returns critically. Note anything it gets wrong about your component — that is the part worth recording.
  3. Record what you ran and what you did with it. This feeds the AI Review Log on whichever assignment this studio is building toward.
