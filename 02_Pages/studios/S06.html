How the session runs

Why a first run that worked tells you so little about the full run

A first run proves your logic is expressible: the steps connect, the tools accept each other's output, a number comes out at the end. That is worth having and it is not what today tests.

The full run tests your assumptions about the data, and the small part of the data you chose hid most of them. You picked one city, one county or one month — and you almost certainly picked a clean one, because a first run needs a part of the data your component could run on.

Four things change when your component runs on all the data, and only the first is the one people expect.

Why a first run that worked tells you so little about the full run
What changesWhat it does to you
VolumeRuntime stops being free. A step that took nine seconds takes forty minutes, and you find out that you cannot iterate any more
VarietyThe records you never saw arrive — a different spelling of a county name, a date in another format, a geometry drawn the other way round
The tailNulls, empty geometries, out-of-range values, gaps in coverage. In a first run these are absent; at full extent they are a percent or two of everything, which is thousands of rows
The environmentMemory, timeouts, disk, a license that limits what you can run at once

Note runtime as a number, in hours, and report it as a project risk. It is the risk most often left out of a status report because it feels like a nuisance rather than a finding — and it is the one that decides whether your component can be re-run once more before the deadline.

What failed on contact

Integration failure is not one thing. Report yours as what you expected to arrive, and what actually did — in those two halves, because the gap between them is the finding.

What failed on contact
KindHow it shows upWhy it happened
FormatThe file opens in your tool and not in theirs, or a field arrives as text that was a numberNobody wrote the format down; each of you used your own default
Coordinate reference systemLayers land in different places, or distances are wrong by a consistent factorA CRS was assumed rather than stated, usually the one your software offered
A missing join keyThe join runs and returns almost nothingTwo identifiers that look alike are not the same identifier, and neither of you checked
An assumption nobody statedNothing errors. The output is wrong, and it is wrong quietlyThe contract said what would be handed over and not what it would mean

The fourth is the dangerous one, and it is the reason this session exists rather than an email. The first three announce themselves. The fourth produces a result that runs, looks reasonable, and is not what anyone thinks it is — and the only place it surfaces is a room where the person who made the assumption and the person who inherited it are both present.
