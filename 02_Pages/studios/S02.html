Before you arrive

  • Bring your ranked component preferences from S01.
  • Re-read your project page. You will be working from it in detail now.

In studio — your component

  1. Claim your component. Negotiate with teammates where preferences overlap; someone has to take the piece nobody ranked first.
  2. Write a one-paragraph definition: what your component produces, and what is explicitly out of scope.
  3. Write your interface contract: what you consume (from whom, in what format, by when) and what you deliver (to whom, in what format, by when).
  4. Draft your Project Pitch from this. It is the next thing you owe — the deadline is in the Next box at the foot of this page.

In studio — the team

  1. Confirm every component has exactly one owner, and that nothing important is unowned.
  2. Assemble every interface contract into one diagram.
  3. Hunt for gaps — anything someone expects to receive that nobody is producing.
  4. Hunt for overlaps — anyone duplicating work.
  5. Confirm your Project Manager. From here they own the interface diagram.

How the session runs

The lecture part is short. Most of today is you at a surface, writing things down.

Claim your component — 20 minutes. Six of you ranked the same component first. Two components nobody ranked at all. And eight proposals that are on neither project's list, which you found independently and which are mostly right. All three get settled out loud, in this room. Nobody leaves without a component name written down.

Define it — 10 minutes. One paragraph: what your component gives the project. One sentence: what it explicitly does not cover, even though somebody might assume it does. The second half is the one that prevents an argument at the integration test.

Write your interface contract — 13 minutes. Who you need something from, and who is waiting on you. Both directions. A form for each handover so nobody has to guess, and a real date on every promise — a date you would defend, not a comfortable one.

Assemble the diagram — 10 minutes. Every contract on one surface. Draw a line from each "I consume" to whoever promised to deliver it. A line that ends nowhere is a gap. A deliverable two people both claim is an overlap. Then confirm your Project Manager, who owns the diagram from here.

Nothing is handed in today. Everything you write is the Project Pitch, which is due Tuesday. Keep all of it.
