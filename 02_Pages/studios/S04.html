In studio — your component

How the session runs

The AI Method Log is graded from here

Project Draft 1 is the first deliverable carrying a graded AI Method Log. It is about the analysis, not the writing. Whatever you used AI for in building this pilot — choosing a projection, picking a geoprocessing tool, drafting a script, reading an error — record it, and record how you checked it. "It ran" is not a check. If you used AI for nothing technical this week, say that honestly and say what you would use it for next week; an honest empty log scores better than an invented full one.

Why saying so now is worth more marks than saying so in November

A pilot that fails is not a bad outcome today. It is the outcome this session is designed to produce cheaply, and reporting it honestly is worth more marks than reporting a success you cannot yet support.

That is not encouragement. It is how the assessment is built, and it is worth understanding rather than trusting.

Why saying so now is worth more marks than saying so in November
When you say itWhat it costs the projectHow it is marked
TodayAn afternoon. Your Project Manager re-plans while there are ten weeks left, and the component gets scoped down, re-approached, or reassignedA finding. You executed a method, produced evidence it does not work, and reported it — which is three of the four things this course assesses
In NovemberThe team's integration, the poster, and somebody else's component that was waiting on your outputA failure to manage a risk you already knew about, and it shows up in the report as a gap nobody had time to close

The thing being graded is the judgment, not the result. "This does not work" on its own is worth nothing — it is the same sentence as giving up. What earns the marks is the sentence with the evidence in it: what you ran, on what slice, what came out, and which specific step is the one that fails.

Three ways of saying the same thing, only one of which is worth anything:

  • Worth nothing. "I could not get it to work."
  • Worth a little. "The imagery is too coarse." Probably true, and nobody can check it or act on it.
  • What you are aiming for. "I ran the classification on one county at 30-meter resolution. Settlement edges under about 60 meters are not separable, and the smallest features the client cares about are around 40. Either we accept that the method misses those, or we need 10-meter imagery, which is available for 2019 onward and not for the 2005 baseline."

The third one is a project decision handed to the Project Manager ready to make. That is what reporting a risk means, and it is why the honest answer today is the profitable one.

Review each other's work, here, now

10 minutes, in class, and it replaces a Canvas peer review. Swap with the person next to you. Read theirs, then talk — do not write a document. You are reviewing Project Draft 1, which was due on Tuesday.

What to look for

  • Could you obtain the same data from this description?
  • Is the fitness-for-use reasoning honest, or optimistic?
  • Does the interface contract match what you expect to receive from this person?
  • Is the roadmap achievable in the weeks remaining?

No written review is submitted for this. What is submitted is what you do next: the change you make because of what you heard, recorded in your AI Method Log or your next draft. Feedback you did not act on is feedback you did not get — and the person who told you it was wrong is sitting next to you, so ask them.

AI use required on this assignment

Using AI on this assignment is required, not optional. You are being assessed on how well you direct and evaluate the tool — the skill employers now assume you have.

  1. Run the Rubber Duck persona on today’s work. Debugging companion — you explain the method until the flaw surfaces.
  2. Read what it returns critically. Note anything it gets wrong about your component — that is the part worth recording.
  3. Record what you ran and what you did with it. This feeds the AI Review Log on whichever assignment this studio is building toward.
