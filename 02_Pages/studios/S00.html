How the session runs

What TAMU AI Chat actually is

Worth two minutes at the start, because the shape of it is the lesson.

  • It is not a model. It is a broker. One interface in front of GPT, Claude, Gemini and Llama at once, run by the A&M System on Google Cloud. You pick the model per prompt, and you can run one prompt against several at the same time and merge the answers.
  • It is built on Open WebUI, the same open-source front end many companies self-host, with a routing layer behind it. Nothing here is bespoke to A&M except the boundary drawn around it.
  • The boundary is the point. Your prompts are not used to train the vendors' models, and it is approved for university data including HIPAA and FERPA. That is why client material may go in here and may not go into the public tool.

Why this looks like your first job

Employers who take this seriously put exactly this in front of their staff: one broker, an approved model list, per-user budgets, and rules about what may be typed in. A&M sorts data into four classification levels and decides which tools may touch which level; every serious employer does the same thing under a different name. Knowing the classification of what you are about to paste is the actual professional skill, and it is a question almost no new graduate thinks to ask.

The Pro tier runs open-weight models on VISION, A&M's own supercomputer, rather than a vendor API — the same build-or-buy decision your employer will be making. A graduate who has only used consumer ChatGPT has not met the constraints of the job.

How to talk to one of these

The full version is the How to talk to one of these page, which you will use all semester. Two habits matter more than the rest.

1. Make it ask rather than assume. A model always produces an answer, because that is what it does. If you left something out, it fills the gap with an assumption you never saw it make, and everything after that is built on the assumption. So make it a standing instruction:

If information is missing, ask me for it rather than assuming. Say explicitly when you are inferring something rather than working from what I gave you.

2. When you are working an idea out, make it interview you. Do not ask for a plan. Ask it to ask you the questions whose answers would most change what it recommends. You are not using it to think for you — you are using it to find the part of your own thinking you have not done.

Almost nobody does the second one, and it is the highest-value move in the whole list.

In studio — the interview drill

You do not have a client project yet, so use something real you actually have to decide in the next few months: a job or internship you want, a graduate program, a trip, a project in another class.

  1. Tell it your task, then: "Do not give me advice yet. Ask me the five questions whose answers would most change what you would recommend, one at a time."
  2. Answer honestly — including "I don't know", which is data.
  3. Write down the one question it asked that you had not thought about. There is almost always one, and that is the whole exercise.
  4. Now let it answer, and notice how far that is from what a one-line prompt would have produced.

If it answers anyway instead of asking, that is a prompting problem worth hitting today: put the constraint first, say "do not" explicitly, and if it still answers, correct it — "You gave me a plan. I asked for questions. Ask me question one." Correcting it mid-conversation is a normal move and most people never try it.

Working with your own files

No installation, no API key and no terminal. It is already in the tool you just logged into.

  • Knowledge collections. Workspace → Knowledge → + → Upload directory pushes a whole folder in. Then type # in any chat to ground the answer in it. The answers come back citing the source file, which is what makes them checkable — and checking is what the AI Method Log grades.
  • Folders. A folder carries its own system prompt and its own knowledge collection, so every chat started inside it inherits both. That is a per-project workspace in about ninety seconds.
  • What it cannot read. There is a size cap per file, a short list of accepted formats, and no OCR at all. A scanned map, a georeferenced PDF, a screenshot of an attribute table — none of it is read. It will still answer about them. That is the single most important thing on this page.

If you want to drive it from your own machine instead of the browser, there is an optional handout — Using TAMU AI from your own computer — covering API keys and the command-line tools. It is genuinely optional and nothing in this course requires it.

Before you leave: pin your chats

An unpinned chat is archived after 60 days of no activity and deleted at 90. Knowledge collections and custom models are deleted after 180 days with nothing using them.

A semester is about 105 days. Work you do in September is inside the deletion window by the time you write the final report.

Hover the chat, click the three dots, choose Pin. Do it for anything connected to your project, today and every week after. Pinned chats are kept forever, and nobody can recover a deleted one — not you, not me, not IT.

The two client projects, in detail

Read the full brief for each on its own Canvas page before Tuesday. Each page carries the question, the work you could own, what the project hands over, and — the part worth your attention — what is still unsettled about it.

Screwworm transmission pathways

  • The barrier held from 1966 until it did not; detections moved north through Central America, into Chiapas, then Zavala County in June 2026.
  • The client asked for an intelligence estimate: competing hypotheses, an evidence matrix, calibrated confidence and named information gaps.
  • The hardest idea in the project: a detection map is a surveillance map. No cases can mean nobody looked.
  • Illicit movement is unobserved by definition — you reason about what would be visible about something designed not to be.
  • Imagery comes through NGA Tearline; the analysis may be considered for Tearline publication, and a Bush School capstone continues it in the spring.

Full brief: the Project: New World Screwworm Transmission Pathways page. Specification status: partial — 4 question(s) still open on it.

Urbanization in African cities

  • Three tasks in sequence: delineate city boundaries from historic imagery, quantify the rates, predict what comes next.
  • The brief says this methodology has not been attempted on an NGA product and could be applied worldwide if it works. There is no answer key.
  • The hardest idea in the project: 'where does the city end' has no natural answer — administrative, built-up, commuting and lit boundaries give four different cities.
  • Spread is not sprawl. Infill, edge expansion and leapfrog are different phenomena with different consequences.
  • The geopolitical half is what the client cares about: infrastructure strain, and external influence through development finance.
  • Data this semester is open and rough. Documented method and honest negative results are what the next cohort inherits.
