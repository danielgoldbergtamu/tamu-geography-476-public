Before you arrive

  • Prepare your section of the presentation. Know your own component cold.

In studio — your component

  1. Present your component to your team. Time it.
  2. Take hostile questions. Every question you cannot answer is homework.
  3. Practice saying “I do not know — here is what would tell us.” It is a strong answer, not a weak one.
  4. Rehearse the answer-first opening: finding first, method second. Whatever the industry calls it, the discipline is the same — do not make a busy listener wait for the point.

In studio — the team

  1. Run the full team presentation end to end, timed.
  2. Take questions as a team. Decide who answers what.
  3. Cut whatever does not fit the time. Something always has to go.

How the session runs

The questions nobody could answer, listed and assigned

The last four minutes are the only part of today that produces a document, and it is the part most likely to be skipped because the room is tired and the clock has run out. Do not skip it.

Every question that went unanswered gets written down, in the words it was asked in. Not a paraphrase, and not a topic — the sentence. "How do you know your detections are not just where somebody happened to be looking?" is a question you can go and work on. "Something about surveillance bias" is a note you will not be able to use on Sunday night.

Each one gets three things beside it.

  • The name of the person who owns it. One name. A question owned by the team is a question owned by nobody, and this is a five-day turnaround.
  • What answering it would take — a number to look up, an assumption to state, a limitation to admit, or a re-run. Deciding this now is what stops somebody spending three days on a question that needed one sentence.
  • Whether it has to be answered before Tuesday, or whether it belongs in the final report. Both are legitimate. A question you cannot resolve by GIS Day is not a hole in your presentation — it is something you say out loud, once, in the form "we do not know that yet, and here is what would tell us."

The list is the agenda for the last three weeks of the course, and it is worth more than the rehearsal that produced it. A question that got asked in this room by a teammate will get asked in the hall by a stranger, and the second time you will have an answer.
