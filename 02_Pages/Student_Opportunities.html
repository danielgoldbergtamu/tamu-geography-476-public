Money that exists to get you there

Presenting somewhere costs money before it earns you anything, and the college has a fund for exactly that. It is listed here because nobody announces it to undergraduates.

Undergraduate Research Program — College of Arts and Sciences
What it pays forThree things: a stipend for an undergraduate doing faculty-supervised research, travel for an undergraduate to present the results of that research at a professional meeting, and materials or equipment needed to do the research. The middle one is the one that gets you to a conference.
How muchRequests are normally under $2,500 per student per semester, and funding may extend beyond a single fiscal year.
Deadlinerolling
How it startsYou do not apply for this one — I do. The proposal goes in from the supervising faculty member, which means the first step is a conversation with me, early enough that there is something to propose. Proposals are accepted on a rolling basis, so there is no deadline to miss and no reason to wait for one.
Before you askTwo conditions worth knowing before you ask. The work has to be faculty-supervised research — your project in this course can qualify, but it has to be set up that way rather than relabeled afterward. And the college expects a supervising faculty member with accessible startup, endowment or IDC funds to cost-share, so the answer depends partly on what I have. Proposals involving first-generation students are especially encouraged, in alignment with the TAMU QEP Aggie Firsts.

About conference deadlines

A conference deadline is usually months before the event. The GEOINT Symposium is in May 2027, which is after most of you graduate — but the student windows open in December and January, while you still have the work from this course in front of you and while I can still write you a reference. That is the point of listing it now rather than in the spring.

If you are thinking about any of these, come and talk to me early. A proposal written the week before a deadline reads like one. I would rather spend twenty minutes on your abstract in October than read a rejection in March.
