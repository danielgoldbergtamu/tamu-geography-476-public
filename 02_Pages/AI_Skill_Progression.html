How your AI skills build across this term

The progression behind every AI requirement in this course

 

Why you are being shown this

Every graded assignment in this course requires AI, and it would be reasonable to assume that means the same task eighteen times. It does not. The instruments stay the same all term; what changes is what counts as doing them well.

The AI Review Log you complete in the last week has exactly the same five fields as the one you complete in the first. A log that earns full points in week four would not earn them in week twelve, and that is deliberate. Here is the whole progression, so you can see where you are and what is coming.

The 6 levels, and they are Bloom's levels

Mapped to Bloom's revised taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001): Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create.. Each level revisits the same instruments at a higher cognitive demand rather than introducing new ones. The AI Review Log a student completes in week fifteen has the same five fields as the one they complete in week one; what changed is what counts as completing it well.

The same instruments, a rising bar

This is the part worth understanding. Nothing new gets introduced late in the term to catch you out — the demand rises on things you already know how to do.

How the bar rises on one instrument: the AI Review Log
Week 1Complete it at all. It carries no points. You are learning the shape of it.
Weeks 2–5Complete it accurately on real work. Name the personas, record the actual prompt, make the changes traceable. One rejection with a stated reason is what mastery looks like at this stage.
Weeks 6–9The comparison starts carrying the points. What did AI catch that you missed, what did you catch that it missed, and where is it simply wrong about this draft?
Weeks 10–12Two or more rejections, with reasons that reference specific content, and at least one identifying something the tool got factually or methodologically wrong. The top of the scale requires you to be right about the subject, not just confident.
Weeks 13–15You build a reviewer role that does not exist yet, and show it found something the published catalog missed.

Where each stage sits

Remember — Week 1

What is being asked of you. Know that these tools exist in a professional, university-protected form; that they fail in characteristic ways; and what the course's instruments ask for.

Components in play: R AI Review Log required, P Named reviewer personas

What shows it worked: The practice log, submitted and scored on the AI Competency scale for the record only, and the week-one diagnostic.

Understand — Weeks 1–2

What is being asked of you. Explain WHY the instruments ask what they ask — why the rejected field carries the most weight, why the peer review order is fixed, why fluent output is not evidence.

Components in play: L Prompt Library embed, W Worked-example reference

What shows it worked: Diagnostic items at this level answered correctly; the S01 discussion of what a demanding prompt looks like against a lazy one.

Apply — Weeks 2–5

What is being asked of you. Use a named persona on real work of their own, with a prompt adapted from the library rather than pasted from it, and record what happened accurately.

Components in play: R AI Review Log required, P Named reviewer personas, L Prompt Library embed, T AI Method Log — AI in the technical work, V Peer review with AI protocol

What shows it worked: Review Logs at rubric level 2–3; the first Method Logs; the S03 exercise in which AI names data sources that do not exist.

Analyze — Weeks 5–9

What is being asked of you. Set the tool's output against something else — their own prior reading, the documentation, a known-good result — and say where the two differ and which is right.

Components in play: V Peer review with AI protocol, R AI Review Log required, T AI Method Log — AI in the technical work

What shows it worked: The provenance split at the top of every peer review; Method Log entries that name what was checked against what.

Evaluate — Weeks 5–12

What is being asked of you. Decide which critiques are warranted and reject the rest with reasons that hold; identify where the tool is factually or methodologically wrong; judge whether a trained model's reported accuracy transfers to this study area.

Components in play: R AI Review Log required, T AI Method Log — AI in the technical work, V Peer review with AI protocol

What shows it worked: Rejection warrant type shifting from stylistic to domain-factual and methodological — indicator I2.

Create — Weeks 5–15

What is being asked of you. Design the scaffolding rather than consume it: build a reviewer role that does not exist yet, and show it found something the published ones did not.

Components in play: P Named reviewer personas, R AI Review Log required, T AI Method Log — AI in the technical work

What shows it worked: The Persona Construction assignment — the transfer task, and the one observation that cannot be produced by following instructions.

The check you took in week one

Its questions are tagged with exactly these levels — the same six levels. That is why your profile came back by topic and by level rather than as a single score: it tells you which part of this progression you were already at when you arrived, and the same check in the last week tells you how far you moved.
