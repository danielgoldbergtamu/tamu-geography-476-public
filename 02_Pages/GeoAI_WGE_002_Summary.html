What does not exist

  • Any automated way, in this workflow, to test whether a rendered map is color-safe. The three tests that work are a grayscale conversion, a color-vision-deficiency simulation and looking at it at delivery size, and all three are performed by a person.

What was NOT built, and why

Read this section first if you read nothing else. The deliverable that was refused is the one you would most likely have produced.

  • Answering "yes, add a rule" without checking whether the maps already made were compliant. The question was about policy and the useful answer was in an artifact. A rule written without opening the map would have been correct, agreed to, and would have left a WCAG 1.4.1 failure on a slide being taught three hours later.
  • Built instead: The map was opened, the defect found, the map fixed, and the rule written from the defect rather than from the principle.

What no check would have caught

The rules and gates this course runs on, and why each one would have passed the version that was wrong.

  • a review that asks whether a figure has alternative text — this one had 263 words of it
  • any check that reads a document rather than the graphic inside it
  • looking at the map on a good monitor with normal color vision

What went wrong while building it

  • The map went onto a teaching slide with two thematic classes separated by hue alone. The classes were chosen as colors — gold for restricted, maroon for a named case — and no second channel was ever considered. It looked correct to the person who drew it, which is the whole difficulty. Caught by The instructor asking whether generated maps needed an accessibility rule of their own..

What to take from it

  • Hue is one of Bertin's visual variables and the weakest for categorical separation. Texture, value, orientation, shape and outline weight are all available and all survive failure modes hue does not.
  • Redundant encoding is the principle: every class carries at least two channels, so losing one still leaves the map readable.
  • A hue-only failure is total, not graceful. When the map's argument IS the contrast between two classes, a reader who cannot separate them gets no argument at all from a figure that looks finished.
  • Test, do not assume: convert to grayscale, simulate deuteranopia, and look at it at the size it will actually be seen.
  • A caption is not a legend. The figure travels into slides, PDFs and pages; the caption often does not travel with it.
  • Non-text contrast at 3:1 applies to map symbology. It is written for exactly this and is remembered only for text.
  • Alternative text does not make a map accessible. The reader harmed by a hue-only map is looking straight at it.

What this means for the job you are about to apply for

The accessibility review in most workplaces looks at the document, not at the map inside it — headings, reading order, alt text, text contrast — and treats a figure as an image needing a description. A map is not an image needing a description; it is an argument made in visual variables, and its accessibility is a property of the symbology. A process that only asks whether a figure has alt text will pass every hue-only map you ever make.

The map itself

Shown here rather than only linked, because a map is the one artifact where seeing it IS understanding it.

What this map shows Four small maps of Texas, one per date, showing counties in two treatments that mean two different things and that differ in PATTERN as well as color, so the distinction survives color blindness and grayscale printing. A hatched gold county is under a movement restriction; a solid maroon county with a heavy dark outline has a confirmed case that somebody published by name. A drawn legend on the figure says both. On 3 June 2026 one county is maroon, Zavala, with one detection recorded and no restriction zone yet. On 12 June, five counties are maroon and twelve are gold in a band across the Hill Country and the border, with nine detections recorded - the only date where the two layers agree, because every detection was named, dated and located. On 1 August, eleven counties are maroon and no county is gold, because forty-four detections had been recorded and the restriction list was reported only as "parts of 22 counties" without naming them. On 26 August, thirteen counties are maroon and twenty-three gold, spreading west into the Trans-Pecos and south to the Rio Grande, with forty-seven detections recorded. The figure establishes that the two records diverge: the number of detections rises far faster than the number of counties anybody can name, and by the last date thirty-four of forty-seven detections have no county in any public text, because the per-case detail is held in a dashboard that publishes no county list. A footer states that a county with no color means no source published a county rather than no cases, that zones and cases are published by different bodies and do not nest, that every zone covers only parts of the counties named while whole counties are drawn, and that the projection is Albers equal area.
