What was asked, in his words

"We obviously need to create a new slide that shows possible places that cattle could be entering the US which are not official border crossings ... with additional restrictions specific to cattle -> must have access to forage, must have access to water, must not have a fence." Then, on leaving for the day: "I want you to build the first pass first, then the full model. If you are able, I want you to take the analysis all the way through to slide deck output. Please take my browser and do the data pulls where you cannot get them from the shells."

What the data turned out to be

Every source that was tried, including the ones that gave nothing. A source that yielded nothing is a finding, not a gap in the record.

What the data turned out to be

Copernicus DEM GLO-90gave what was needed — Twelve 1-degree tiles, 4.9 MB each. GLO-30 was available at the same address pattern and deliberately not taken: 90 m is already finer than the 231 m analysis grid, and 30 m would have been 480 MB to gain nothing.

Navigating to an Overpass URL does not download; Overpass sends a permissive CORS header, so the query was run by fetch from a page and the result downloaded as a blob.

What does not exist

  • Ranch fencing. It is the real constraint on the ground and it is essentially unmapped, which is why the barrier term carries the lowest weight and why the deck ends by asking TAHC and USDA for it.
  • Anything that would validate the surface directly. Tick-rider stray capture locations and fever tick outbreak premises are not public, so the validation had to be built out of something withheld instead.
  • River stage as a time series. The fordable set moves seasonally and this model does not know it.
  • Municipio-level Mexican case counts. They sit inside a Power BI report whose visuals re-render from cache; three interception attempts returned nothing, and that state is written down in 06_Working_Notes/border_data_dig.md.

What was NOT built, and why

Read this section first if you read nothing else. The deliverable that was refused is the one you would most likely have produced.

  • Plotting interior Border Patrol checkpoints. GAO reported in 2022 that Border Patrol keeps no complete or reliable data on them, so a dot would be an invented coordinate. What is drawn instead is the line the regulation defines - 100 air miles from the boundary, 8 CFR 287.1 - inside which the checkpoints sit.
  • Weighting roads by measured traffic, as literally asked. Nobody publishes segment flow for the Mexican network, and US car counts are the wrong variable for cattle. The crossings carry the measured weight and the roads carry class only, and the map says so.

What no check would have caught

The rules and gates this course runs on, and why each one would have passed the version that was wrong.

  • any checker here - none of them ask whether a model discriminates, and a flat surface passes everything
  • residuals or goodness of fit - a weighted overlay has none, which is why the validation had to come from data held out
  • the accessibility gates, for a class that disappears under a projector while passing every contrast rule on a good screen

What went wrong while building it

  • The first render of the ranked river drew the bottom decile pale. A tone chosen on a good screen. Caught by A simulated projector wash. The class vanished entirely, and a vanished class does not read as "low rank", it reads as "no river there at all". Every checker passed before and after..
  • The evidence slide printed its finding through the DATA WE USED heading. A fixed-height text box sized for a shorter finding. Caught by The rendered page. The fix then printed a source line off the bottom edge, for the same reason - sizing the figure first and hoping the words fit..
  • 205 MB of rasters written into the versioned content tree. Not reading the rule already in config/layout.yaml, which says in as many words that allowing .geojson is not a license to put a raster archive in a git repository. Caught by layout_check, at 135 problems..

What to take from it

  • A model that returns "almost anywhere is fine" has not failed to find an answer. It has found that the binding constraints are elsewhere, and that is a result with a direction in it.
  • Hold something back BEFORE you build, so the validation cannot be tuned toward it. The three legal ports were never an input, and the model ranked them 406th, 396th and 311th of 411.
  • Then run the check that could destroy your result. "It is just distance from town" was the obvious objection; correlation with distance to the nearest port is -0.036, and with bank relief -0.61.
  • Choose the dataset by what the question needs, not by what is familiar. NLCD is the obvious US land cover and it stops at the river.
  • Aggregate a categorical raster by COUNTING, on a whole-number block of source cells. Resampling categories any other way invents classes.
  • Weight what is measured and refuse to weight what is not. A line width read as a flow has misled the reader through the map, not the caption.
  • Verify an automated edit by reversing it. Every lifted literal was resolved back through the register and compared to the original, in order.

What this means for the job you are about to apply for

The deliverable a client remembers is the map, and what makes it worth anything is the sentence underneath saying what it cannot support. This model names its own weakest term, states that a low barrier score means nobody mapped a fence, and puts the frame - surveillance prioritisation, not a route guide - on the face of the figure rather than in the speaker notes.
