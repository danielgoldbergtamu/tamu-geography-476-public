GEOG 476 — GIS Practicum

Fall 2026 · T/Th 12:45–2:00 PM · CSA 303 · Dr. Dan Goldberg

 

This semester, your client is a federal agency

You will not be inventing a project. You will be working a real problem brought to this department by USDA and the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency — problems those agencies are actively trying to solve right now.

Students on these projects may be offered a trip to Washington, DC to brief senior officials on their findings.

That is not a reward bolted onto the end of a course. It is the reason the course is built the way it is. Everything you will do this semester — the analytic tradecraft, the briefing format, the hard questions in Thursday studio — is preparation for standing in a room and defending work that someone is going to act on.

Nobody expects you to solve these

These are hard problems that professional analysts are working on with better data than you will have. You are not being asked to finish them.

You are being asked to do the initial exploration — build the framework, establish what the method should be, find out what is knowable and what is not, and hand it forward to the students who come after you. Future 476 cohorts and graduate students in INTA 670 will pick up where you stop. Saying clearly what you could not determine is a finding, not a failure.

Three things that are different this year

1. AI use is required, not permitted

Not tolerated, not allowed with disclosure — required, on every assignment. The one thing that stays yours is the writing itself: this is a W course, so the draft you submit has to be your own words.

What is graded is whether you can direct AI well and judge its output critically — because that is the skill employers now assume you have. You will use AI as a reviewer: a hard reviewer, a non-technical reviewer, a hostile questioner. And you will document where you overruled it, which is the part that shows you were thinking.

2. You work in a team, but you own a piece

Each client problem belongs to a team. Within it, you own one defined component that the team’s answer depends on. One student per team serves as Project Manager — a role that looks easier than it is.

3. Thursdays are studio

Tuesdays are lectures. Thursdays you build. Bring your laptop. Each studio moves your project through one stage of the professional workflow — framing, data discovery, pilot analysis, validation, cartography, briefing.

One expectation, stated plainly

This is a capstone, and it is the last course that stands between you and doing this work for money. The projects are real, the clients are real, and the possibility of briefing them in Washington is real. What you get out of this semester will be closely related to what you put into it.
