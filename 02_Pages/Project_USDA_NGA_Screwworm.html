This is a real request from a real client

In August 2026, RDML Michael Schmoyer — who leads USDA’s response to New World screwworm — visited Texas A&M and asked whether this department could provide unclassified geospatial intelligence support to his effort. He has raised it with the Secretary of Agriculture, who is supportive.

Commercial satellite imagery comes through the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency’s Tearline program. Your analysis may be considered for publication through Tearline. A parallel Bush School capstone runs in the spring using the methodology you develop this fall.

You may be offered a trip to Washington to brief senior officials

Students on the client projects may be offered a trip to Washington, DC, to brief senior federal officials on their work.

This is not a field trip and it is not a reward for good grades. It is why this course spends time on writing for decision-makers and on defending your work under questioning. The audience is real, in the room, with limited time; the questions are hard and answered live, without notes, by the student who did the work.

Which means the hostile question-and-answer sessions in studio are not an exercise. They are rehearsal.

The situation

New World screwworm (Cochliomyia hominivorax) is a parasitic fly whose larvae infest the living tissue of warm-blooded animals. The United States eradicated it in 1966 and held it behind a sterile-fly barrier for decades. That barrier has failed.

Established northward spread
PeriodLocation
2023 onwardCentral America, including Guatemala
November 2024 onwardChiapas, Mexico
June 2026 onwardZavala County, Texas — first US detection in decades

Further detections followed in Texas and New Mexico. Southern ports closed to livestock trade, with phased reopening beginning at Douglas, Arizona on 24 August 2026. Reports indicate livestock movement has contributed to the geographic spread.

Your task

The core question

Determine whether the geographic and livestock-movement environment from Guatemala through southern Mexico and toward South Texas provides plausible pathways for an infected animal to reach the United States without being detected.

The question you are being asked

What geographic, environmental, livestock-network, and surveillance factors could plausibly contribute to the movement of New World screwworm-infested cattle from southern Mexico toward South Texas, and what evidence supports competing explanations for the June 2026 detections in Zavala County?

The supporting questions

  1. Where have New World screwworm detections occurred over time?
  2. How has the geographic distribution of New World screwworm changed?
  3. Where are important cattle-producing areas?
  4. What transportation networks connect cattle-producing regions?
  5. Where are livestock markets and aggregation points?
  6. How does the movement network intersect the geographic expansion of New World screwworm?
  7. What environmental conditions could influence transmission?
  8. Where does surveillance coverage appear relatively strong or weak?
  9. What evidence supports animal movement as a mechanism of spread?
  10. What alternative explanations exist?
  11. What additional information would most reduce uncertainty?

The deliverable is an Intelligence Estimate — not a map

Read this twice

The client brief states it plainly: “Final Product would be an Intelligence Estimate, not merely a GIS map.”

A beautiful map that does not answer the question, weigh competing explanations, and state its own confidence has not done the job. Cartography serves the assessment here; it is not the assessment.

Estimate question: What is the most plausible explanation for the appearance of NWS in Zavala County, and where could NWS appear next?

Why take this project

Every project in this course teaches GIS. This one teaches you to reason under uncertainty for a decision-maker, which is a different and rarer skill — and the thing that separates a person who makes maps from a person who is trusted to say what the maps mean.

Skills nobody else graduates with

You will produce a genuine intelligence estimate — competing hypotheses developed and tested against evidence, confidence levels that are calibrated and justified rather than asserted, and explicit reasoning about activity that is unobserved by definition. Illicit livestock movement leaves no record; you have to reason about what would be visible about something invisible. No other class in this degree teaches that, and it is not something you can pick up from a tutorial.

The problem is live, and it is expensive

New World screwworm is not a case study invented for a syllabus. It reemerged in Mexico, the June 2026 detections in Zavala County are real, southern ports closed to livestock, and the US cattle industry is measured in the tens of billions of dollars. Nobody currently knows the answer to the question you are being asked. That is why they are asking.

Name another graduate who can say this

“I produced a GEOINT intelligence estimate for a federal client, with competing hypotheses and calibrated confidence, and briefed it.” Say that in an interview at USDA APHIS, at the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, at the Department of Homeland Security, or at any of the contractors that staff them, and watch what happens to the conversation. These are clearance-track roles and they are among the best-paid work a geospatial graduate can walk into.

Your work does not stop in December

You are not expected to solve this in one semester and nobody will pretend otherwise. You are building the framework that the next cohort of GEOG 476 students and the graduate students in INTA 670 pick up and carry forward. Documenting a method well enough that a stranger can re-run it, and naming precisely what is still unknown, is the deliverable — and it means your name is on the foundation rather than on a file that closes when grades post.

The hard parts

  1. A detection map is a surveillance map. No cases in a county may mean no screwworm — or no one looking. Conflating those produces a confident wrong answer, and it is the first thing a USDA reviewer will check.
  2. Illicit movement is unobserved by definition. The routes that matter most are the ones nobody records. You must reason about what would be visible about an activity designed not to be.
  3. Three competing hypotheses are required, not optional. Picking a favorite and arguing for it is not analysis.
  4. Confidence must be justified. “High confidence” needs a stated reason.
  5. Your audience is a decision-maker. An analysis a policy audience cannot act on has failed at its actual job, however good the GIS.

Ethical dilemmas this project raises

Your Ethics Essay should be about a dilemma from your own project. These are real ones this work produces — use one, or use something you hit yourself. If you have a stronger case from somewhere else, the assignment page explains how to use it instead.

Dilemmas arising from this project
The dilemmaWhat makes it hard
Publishing the surveillance gapsYour map shows where surveillance is weak. At full resolution it tells ranchers and agencies where to look harder — and it tells anyone moving cattle illicitly exactly where not to be seen. Do you publish it at that resolution, and if not, who decides what is coarse enough?
Inference that implicates peopleConcluding that illicit movement is the likely mechanism names routes. Routes imply operators, communities and border crossings, and none of them get to respond. How confident should you be before an analysis effectively accuses someone?
Confidence with consequences attachedA policy audience may restrict movement or close ports partly on the strength of your estimate, and livelihoods turn on that. What confidence threshold justifies saying it out loud — and is understating your confidence to stay safe its own kind of failure?

AI on this project

AI is required on this project in two distinct ways, and both are graded. Neither is optional and neither is a formality.

What AI is required for on this project
On what you writeEvery deliverable carries an AI Review Log — the personas you ran, the actual prompts, what you changed, and what you rejected and why.
On the work itselfEvery project deliverable also carries an AI Method Log — where AI entered the technical work you own, what you asked, what it proposed, and how you verified it. Data discovery, projections, tool and parameter choice, scripting, debugging, quality checks. If you are the Project Manager, the same applies to the plan, the dependencies, the integration and the risk register.

The rule that governs both

You may not adopt a method you cannot defend independently of the tool that suggested it.

This client will act on what you hand them. A projection chosen because a model suggested it, a dataset cited because a model named it, or a statistic reported because a model computed it — none of those survive contact with someone who checks. Every source AI names must be opened before it is cited. It invents plausible dataset names and dead URLs, confidently, and that is the most common way this goes wrong.
