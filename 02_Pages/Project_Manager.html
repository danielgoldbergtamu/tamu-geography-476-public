Every project runs two teams doing the same work, and every level of that structure has someone accountable for it. This page is what those people are accountable for.

Why two teams, and why that needs managing

Two teams on one project is not duplication for its own sake. It is replication — the only means of independent validation this course can offer. Where both teams reach the same answer you have a defensible result; where they diverge you have the most interesting finding in the project, and a client report that says so is far stronger than one that never noticed.

That only works if somebody owns the comparison. Nobody inside a team can, because they are inside it.

The three roles

The three roles
RoleScopeOwns
Team Project ManagerOne teamThat team's plan, its board, its status, its interfaces
Program ManagerBoth teams on one projectThe comparison, the reconciled report, and what the client sees
Component ownerOne componentTheir own work, and telling their PM the truth about it early

On the name. The higher role is called Program Manager because that is what the level above a project manager is called in professional practice — federal, consulting and industry alike. A program is a set of related projects run for one outcome, which is exactly what two teams on one client question are. If you see "super-manager" anywhere in older material, this is the role it meant.

Both Project Managers and the Program Manager are held all semester. The role does not rotate. Continuity is the point: a manager who has watched a component drift for six weeks knows things a rotating manager cannot.

What a Project Manager actually does

  • Keeps the board current. Not as an administrative chore — as the artifact the team plans from.
  • Knows the state of every component, including the ones nobody has mentioned this week. Silence is the signal worth chasing.
  • Owns the interfaces. When one component's output is another's input, the PM owns the contract between them and the date it is due.
  • Escalates early. A component that is behind is a project risk. A component that is behind and hidden is a project failure.

What the Program Manager does that a Project Manager cannot

  • Runs the comparison. Where do the two teams agree, where do they diverge, and is the divergence a method difference, a data difference, or a mistake?
  • Owns the reconciled report. One report per project, not two stapled together. Where the teams disagree and neither is wrong, that goes in the report as a finding.
  • Moves work between teams. If one team loses a component and the other has it covered, that is the redundancy working — but only if somebody notices and says so.

When somebody disappears

This is the part the structure exists for, so it is stated plainly.

If a team member stops contributing, their work does not stop being owed. The Project Manager either does it or reassigns it. If the Project Manager cannot absorb it, the Program Manager does it or moves it to the other team. At no level is "their component did not get done" an acceptable report to a client.

This is not a threat and it is not unusual — it is what the role is. People withdraw, get ill, and change plans, and the reason professional projects survive that is that somebody is accountable for the gap rather than surprised by it.

The board

Every manager builds and maintains a project tracking board — Kanban or an equivalent that shows what is in flight, what is blocked and what is done. The tool is yours to choose. The requirement is that it is current, that the whole team can see it, and that it is legible to somebody who is not on your team.

See the tutorial for building one with an AI assistant, which is itself an exercise in the automation this course is teaching: a board you rebuild by hand every week is a board you will stop maintaining in week six.

Thursday status

Managers must be ready to demonstrate project status in any Thursday studio. Not a prepared presentation — the live board, and three answers:

  1. What moved this week?
  2. What is blocked, and who owns unblocking it?
  3. What is at risk of not being finished, and what is the plan?

It is called on without notice, which is deliberate. A board that is only accurate on the day you are asked is not a board; it is a performance.

How the role is assessed

Managers are assessed on the same components as everyone else, and the management work is evidence in Engagement — the board's currency, the honesty of status reporting, and whether problems surfaced early or late. Holding the role is not a substitute for technical work, and it is not extra credit for doing paperwork.
