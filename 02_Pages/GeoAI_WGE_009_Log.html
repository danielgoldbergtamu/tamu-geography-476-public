1. The trap, named before any data is opened

The naive form of this analysis is: measure how far each detection is from the nearest aggregation point, compare that against randomly scattered points, find the detections are closer, declare a relationship.

That result is close to guaranteed and it would be worthless. Detections and markets are both where the cattle are. Uniform random points over the map are a null that essentially no real spatial pattern could fail to beat, so beating it measures nothing. The "finding" would be a picture of cattle density with two different labels on it.

So the null has to hold cattle constant. Every random draw in this log is weighted by the GLW4 herd surface already on disk from WGE-007, which turns the question into the one worth asking:

Given where the cattle are, are cases nearer to aggregation points than chance would put them?

This is the same problem the SQ6 plan flagged about surveillance effort, and it gets the same treatment: put the confounder in the null, not in the discussion.

2. What incidence data actually exists, at what resolution

Two sources, already captured, at two different resolutions - and the analysis is built to match each one rather than pretending both are points.

Areal, n = 30 states. content/projects/border_movement.yaml carries SENASICA's per-state counts as published by APHIS on 1 September 2026: 40,392 cumulative cases, 1,941 active, across 30 states. Chiapas 7,569 and Oaxaca 5,181 at the top; Sonora 21 at the bottom.

One-dimensional, n = 398 cases. content/projects/aphis_mexico_cases.csv is APHIS's list of cases within 100 miles of the United States, September 2025 to August 2026. Each case carries a date, a state, a species, an active/inactive flag, whether sterile flies were dropped on it, and an approximate distance from the US border in miles.

That distance is a real spatial coordinate - just a one-dimensional one. Cases run from 0.5 to 100 miles out, median 82. Four states only: Nuevo Leon 256, Coahuila 76, Tamaulipas 59, Chihuahua 7. Species is mostly Bovine (234) but by no means only - 92 Canine, 38 Caprine, 14 Equine.

The selection effect, stated before it can flatter anything

The 100-mile ceiling is a property of the list, not of the outbreak. APHIS publishes cases within 100 miles of the border; the maximum observed value is exactly 100. Any apparent fall-off past that distance is the edge of the sampling frame. Nothing in this log reads the truncation as a finding, and every distance test is run inside 0-100 miles only.

There is no point-located case data. Neither publisher releases coordinates. So no map in this log will draw a case as a dot at a place, because that dot would be an invention.

3. Going back into the Power BI report, and getting in

The 1 September dig note listed three approaches and said all three returned nothing. Two of them work.

Expanding the matrix works. The state rows carry .expandCollapseButton.clickable, and the glyph inside says which state the row is in: pbi-glyph-explore-content-single for collapsed, pbi-glyph-collapse-content-single for expanded. Clicking one loads that state's municipios. Zacatecas opened to 43 of them, Pinos 28 down to a long tail of ones.

The querydata interception works too, and the earlier failure is explained. The hook captured nothing last time because it was installed and then nothing re-queried - switching pages re-renders from cache. Expanding a row forces a genuine re-query. Install the fetch hook on the top document and on the same-origin iframe, THEN click an expand button, and the responses arrive. Four came back, 5,140 to 8,715 bytes, each a data window keyed SeguimientoGBG_CPA.ESTADO / SeguimientoGBG_CPA.MUNICIPIO.

Both techniques are now written into 06_Working_Notes/border_data_dig.md so that the next person does not spend the four hours a third time.

The wall that is still standing

The matrix stops feeding after about ten states. Setting scrollTop on .mid-viewport and dispatching a synthetic scroll grows scrollHeight from 1,440 to 2,800 px and then stops. Ten states came out complete, and they are, in order: Zacatecas, Nayarit, Oaxaca, Yucatan, Puebla, Hidalgo, Chiapas, San Luis Potosi, Queretaro, Quintana Roo, Jalisco.

And the reason none of it is used

The matrix is sorted by descending active cases. So the ten states that came out are the ten states with the most cases. That is a sample selected on the response variable. Correlating facility density against a case dataset that only exists where the cases are would produce a relationship by construction, and the relationship would be an artifact of the scroll bar.

The capture is on disk at _Data/incidence/senasica_powerbi_municipio_capture.json - 143 matrix rows and the four raw bodies - and it contributes nothing to any number in this log. tools/incidence/fetch_inputs.py lists it under CAPTURED AND DELIBERATELY NOT USED, because a file sitting in a data directory implies it was used unless the record says otherwise.

Municipio geometry is no longer the blocker the old note said it was: GeoNames ADM2 centroids came in with WGE-008 and tools/markets/geocode_rastros.py already resolves Mexican municipio names against them. If somebody gets the Casos acumulados page out - 40,392 cases rather than 1,941 - the point-level version of this analysis is a short step.

4. What was actually tested

Three analyses, one per resolution, all in tools/incidence/relate.py, seed 20260902, 999 simulations.

A - areal, 30 states. Case rate per 100k cattle against facility density and against published monthly slaughter capacity per head, by Spearman rank correlation. Then Moran's I on the log rate, queen contiguity from the state polygons, tested by permutation.

B - the border axis, 398 cases. Cases, cattle and facilities placed on the same 0-100 mile scale, restricted to the same four states and the same band, and compared by two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov. Restricting all three to the same band matters: comparing a case distribution truncated at 100 miles against a cattle distribution that is not would have manufactured the finding.

C - Monte Carlo. Each case is represented by the cattle-weighted cells in its own state at its reported distance from the border, and the null keeps the state but redraws the distance from the cattle distribution. The comparison asks one thing: does knowing how far from the border a case was reported put it nearer an aggregation point than a random cow in the same state?

5. The answer is no, in three different ways

A. Across the 30 states, nothing

facility density vs case rate rho 0.064 p 0.737 capacity per head vs case rate rho -0.092 p 0.630 raw facilities vs raw cases rho 0.173 p 0.362 raw cattle vs raw cases rho 0.214 p 0.257

Not weak. Absent. And the last line is worth its own sentence: even cattle does not explain cases at this scale. The wave is moving through the country largely regardless of how many animals are standing in its way.

What is there is space itself:

Moran's I, log case rate 0.448 p 0.001 (expected -0.034, 67 pairs)

A contiguous southern block - Chiapas, Oaxaca, Yucatan, Quintana Roo, Veracruz. Where you are on the map predicts the case rate. How many aggregation points you have does not. That is the signature of an invasion front.

B. Along the border, the gradients run opposite

median distance from the border facilities 26 miles cattle 54 miles cases 82 miles

KS cases vs cattle D 0.317 p 2.5e-35 KS cases vs facilities D 0.564 p 4.3e-08

band cases per 100k cattle facilities 0- 20 mi 6 1.65 11 20- 40 mi 26 6.55 6 40- 60 mi 69 17.02 3 60- 80 mi 83 19.71 1 80-100 mi 211 44.10 6

The incidence rate climbs by a factor of 27 as you move AWAY from the border, while the facilities are concentrated on it.

C. The direct test, and why its p-value is not the point

observed mean distance to nearest facility 43.23 km cattle-weighted null 41.58 km (95% CI 40.78-42.31) difference +1.65 km p 0.001

Significant, and pointing away from facilities rather than towards them. And 1.65 km is nothing. With 398 cases the test detects a difference far smaller than it can mean. It is written on the slide as a non-finding with a p-value attached, which is the honest description of it, and it is the one number here most likely to be quoted out of context by somebody who read only the p.

6. The obvious objection, tested instead of argued about

The first thing anyone will say about a gradient that thins towards the border is that it is not ecology but INTERVENTION: USDA drops sterile flies, and drops them at the border first.

The case list records which cases got a release, so the objection is answerable from the data:

cases with a sterile release 135 median 82 miles cases without 263 median 81 miles KS D 0.096 p 0.355

No difference. The border gradient is not the sterile-fly program. That does not prove the gradient is ecological - surveillance effort could still vary with distance in ways nothing here measures - but it removes the specific competing explanation that was easiest to reach for.

7. What this means, which is not what a "no" usually means

A null result here is more useful than a positive one would have been, and it changes the operational advice rather than removing it.

Today, a surveillance plan that prioritized markets and rastros would be looking in the wrong place. The epidemic is propagating as a front, and the front is 82 miles from the border while the facilities are at 26.

But that is a statement about timing, not about safety. Aggregation is precisely the mechanism that turns a front into a jump: it is where animals from many origins stand together on one day, and it is what lets an infestation skip the intervening ground rather than crawl through it. The facilities sit ahead of the wave. The right reading is that they are a FORWARD risk rather than a current explanation, and that is a different plan from either "watch the markets" or "ignore the markets".

The reproducibility check that failed, and what it turned out to be

On the first check, two of the three derived files came back byte-identical and incidence_relationship.json did not. Three runs afterwards were identical to each other, which is the worst kind of bug report: a failure that will not recur.

It was chased rather than shrugged at, and it has a cause, and the cause is funny enough to be worth the space. The spelling gate fixed a key name inside the generator.

language_check --fix ran between the baseline and the comparison. Among the nine spellings it corrected was neighbour_pairs, a dictionary key in relate.py that is written straight into the JSON as the count of adjacent state pairs behind Moran's I. British to American, neighbour to neighbor: one byte shorter. The baseline file said "neighbour_pairs", the fresh run wrote "neighbor_pairs", and the checksums disagreed by exactly that.

Nothing was wrong with the analysis. Every number was identical. But the general hazard is real and worth naming: a text-fixing gate that edits SOURCE can change the SCHEMA a generator emits, and the artifact then looks non-reproducible when only its labels moved. A rebuild after any prose gate runs is comparing two different programs, not two runs of one.

Four consecutive runs are now byte-identical, and tools/incidence/fetch_inputs.py prints an input hash so the next person can tell a data change from a label change without repeating this.

10. Addendum, same day: "why did we not run this for the US as well?"

Asked by the instructor after the log was closed. It is a fair question and the figure did not answer it on its face, which is the strongest kind of evidence that it should have.

The short answer was n, and the short answer was incomplete

40,392 Mexican cases against 47 American ones. But "not enough data" turned out to be true of two tests and false of the third, and the third produced the most instructive result in either country.

What does not transfer, and why:

  • The areal rank correlation. Mexico had case rates spanning 21 to 7,569 across 30 states. Texas has 13 counties with detections and 241 without. A rank correlation on that is a correlation on a near-constant.
  • Moran's I. Thirteen adjacent counties out of 254 is what an index case looks like. It would return a large number meaning "the first cases were near each other", which is true of every outbreak and says nothing about markets.
  • The border-distance axis. It does not exist. APHIS publishes miles-from-border for the MEXICAN cases only.

What does transfer: the permutation test, at both the county and the cell level. tools/incidence/relate_us.py, 9,999 simulations, same seed.

The naive result, which is enormous and wrong

detected counties, cattle-weighted mean to nearest market 185.8 km cattle-weighted null 58.5 km difference +127.3 km p 0.0001 smallest difference the test could resolve 36.2 km

Three and a half times the detection threshold. Read on its own it says the aggregation network explains the American outbreak.

The confound, and the test that finds it

The detections are near the Mexican border because that is where the outbreak arrives from. The markets are far from that border because of WGE-008's finding - no approved market within 129 miles of the river east of El Paso. So "the detections are far from markets" may be nothing more than "the detections are near the border", said in different words.

The way to find out is to hold border distance constant. For each detected county, draw the null county from those within 60 km of its own distance to the boundary, still weighted by cattle:

detected counties 185.8 km border-matched null 208.4 km difference -22.6 km p 0.174

The effect vanishes, and slightly reverses. Among counties equally close to the border there is no market signal at all.

detected counties sit 110 km from the border Texas cattle sit 453 km from the border

Why this is a measurement and not a shrug

The matched test could resolve a 36 km difference. It found 23 km in the other direction. "No significant difference" and "we could not have seen one" are different sentences here, and this is the first.

What the two countries now say together

Mexico: 40,392 cases, 30 states, no correlation with facility density (rho 0.06) and a strong spatial autocorrelation instead (Moran's I 0.448).

Texas: 47 detections, 13 counties, an apparently enormous market effect that is entirely accounted for by proximity to the border.

Two data regimes, two methods, one answer. The aggregation network is not where the disease is. And in the American case the reason is now visible and uncomfortable: the disease arrived exactly along the stretch where the market network does not reach, which is also the stretch where no federal inspection sees an animal.

The methodological point, which outranks the finding

The first American test is the exact mistake this log was designed to avoid, committed at a different scale. Section 1 says a uniform-random null is one that any spatial pattern beats, so the null was weighted by cattle. That fixed the cattle confound and left the border confound standing, and the border confound was worth 127 km.

A null model does not correct for confounders in general. It corrects for the ones you put in it. Every test needs the question asked again: what else is true of the places where the events happened?
