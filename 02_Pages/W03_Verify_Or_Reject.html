Below is a project plan an AI produced when asked to plan the screwworm transmission component of this course's client project. It has not been edited.

What you do

  1. Read it once, straight through. Do not stop. It will look reasonable — that is the exercise.
  2. Read it again and mark every line you could not check against the brief, a real date, a real price, or a person who would know.
  3. Agree on the worst line — the one you would refuse to put your name on.
  4. Write one sentence: what is wrong with it, and how you would have caught it.

A check is not "it sounds right" and it is not "it ran." Executing is not evidence.

Project plan: New World screwworm transmission pathway analysis

Prepared for: USDA · Duration: 14 weeks · Team: 5 analysts

Phase 1 — Data acquisition (Weeks 1–2)

  1. Download the USDA APHIS National Screwworm Surveillance Layer (updated daily, public REST endpoint) for all confirmed detections 2022–2026.
  2. Acquire Mexican SENASICA cattle movement permits as a point dataset. Available through the SENASICA open data portal at no cost.
  3. Obtain NAIP 4-band imagery at 0.6 m for the Texas border counties for winter 2026.
  4. Pull daily minimum temperature from PRISM at 800 m resolution.

Estimated cost: $0. All sources are open.

Phase 2 — Environmental suitability (Weeks 3–5)

  1. Reproject all layers to NAD83 Texas Statewide Mapping System for consistency.
  2. Build a MaxEnt suitability surface using the detection points as presence records and temperature, humidity and land cover as predictors.
  3. Validate with a 70/30 train–test split. Target AUC > 0.85.

Phase 3 — Movement pathway modeling (Weeks 6–9)

  1. Construct a least-cost path network between Mexican origin municipalities and Texas destination markets, weighting by road class and suitability.
  2. Calibrate travel cost using average cattle transport speed of 45 km/h.
  3. Cross-reference against the USDA approved cattle market list to identify likely entry points.

Phase 4 — Synthesis and delivery (Weeks 10–14)

  1. Integrate all five analyst components into a single geodatabase.
  2. Produce a risk surface, a set of pathway maps, and a technical report.
  3. Deliver to the client in Week 14.

Risks

  • Data may be delayed.
  • Team members may fall behind schedule.
  • Analysis may be more complex than anticipated.
  • Stakeholder requirements may change.

What you hand in

One sentence, in Attendance 1 (this module, text entry): the line you would refuse to put your name on, and how you would have caught it.
