What this session covers

  • Teams and components announced, and the Project Manager for each.
  • What a project management plan actually contains — and why every organization uses a slightly different set of components. Three elements, four, five, ten: the number matters less than whether the plan survives contact with reality.
  • Budgeting a project: people, time, data, software, and the costs students routinely forget.
  • Interface contracts — the promise you make to your teammates about what you will hand them, in what format, by when.
  • Spatial data standards, national and international, and why they exist. Format, coordinate reference system, metadata standards, published specifications. A dataset without documented standards is a dataset you cannot defend.
  • What the Project Manager owns, and what they do not. It is not the easy option and it is not the boss.
  • Doing GIS with AI, and the rule that governs it. Where AI actually helps in a geospatial workflow — data discovery, coordinate systems, tool and parameter choice, scripting, debugging, quality assurance, cartographic critique — and where it is confidently, fluently wrong. Worked live in ArcGIS Pro: asking it to choose a projection and watching what it gets wrong; asking it to name a data source and checking whether the source exists; asking it to explain a geoprocessing parameter and finding the version it invented. The rule: you may not adopt a method you cannot defend independently of the tool that suggested it. This is what the AI Method Log records, and it is required on every project deliverable from Draft 1 onward.
  • The same rule for project managers, applied to the plan rather than the analysis. Ask it where the schedule breaks if one component slips; ask it what dependencies are unstated; then check whether the risks it names are yours or generic.
