What this session covers

  • The kinds of professional writing you will actually produce: reports, proposals, emails, executive summaries, technical documentation, and the resume and cover letter you write in a few weeks.
  • What separates professional writing from student writing. The reader is busy, did not ask for it, and will stop.
  • Common failures, named: burying the point, hedging instead of concluding, passive constructions that hide who did what, and padding to reach a length.
  • Writing short. Every assignment in this course now has an exact length target and it is graded. Anyone can say something in unlimited space; saying it in the space you were given is the skill.
  • Cutting. How to remove fifteen percent without losing the argument, and why AI is bad at this in a specific and predictable way — it pads on request and cuts badly.
  • Editing your own work, using the draft you just submitted.

How the session runs

First, we finish Thursday

Thursday ran out of room before the pitch queue did. The presenters we did not reach go first today, same rules: sixty seconds each, then sixty seconds of feedback, scored by a partner against the four Project Pitch criteria.

If you are presenting today, your partner writes your review today. If you were the partner for somebody who has not presented yet, you write theirs today — which is why Engagement 3 moved.

What is due in this class

Two things, and this is the meeting they are due in.

  1. Engagement 3 — Sep 22. Your review of your partner's presentation: four scores, a reason for each, one thing that worked and one thing to change. Photograph your page before you hand it over. It moved from Thursday to tonight so that students whose partners present today can actually write it.
  2. Project Draft 1 — Sep 29. Four pages plus your first graded AI Method Log, built on the source inventory, standards, profile and license work you were set on Thursday.

Before you arrive
