What this session covers

  • Why this course exists. It is not a lecture course with a project bolted on. It is a semester of professional work with the instruction arranged around it.
  • The syllabus, analyzed rather than read aloud — what is graded and what each
  • What is required and carries no points at all. The GIS Day one-minute
  • How this course differs from a traditional class: you will be doing work nobody knows the answer to, for people outside this university.
  • The Rejection Drill, run in class today. Six AI suggestions — the numbered
  • AI on the project work, stated plainly. Using AI is required here, not
  • The client projects, in summary. Enough to know what each one is asking, who is asking it, and what kind of work it would be. The full briefs are on Canvas and we go through them properly on Thursday.
  • The GIS + AI Knowledge Check, taken in class if there is time or at home before Thursday. Twenty questions about working with these tools, running from straightforward to genuinely hard. It carries no points. It counts for participation and nothing else, and it cannot affect your grade in any other way. Its job is to tell me where to pitch this course — the same thing a placement check does at the start of a mathematics class. Canvas grades it immediately and explains every answer, and you take the same check again in the last week so you can see what changed.

How the session runs

The names on everything

W01 is week one. S00 is studio zero. Tuesdays are lectures and carry a W; Thursdays are studios and carry an S. The two sequences are numbered separately, so S00 sits inside week one — it is the studio where everybody gets their machine able to do the AI work, and it has to come before everything else.

Bring a laptop to every studio

The studio sessions are where you do the work rather than watch it, so a machine you can install things on is the one thing to have with you on a Thursday.

The attendance rule, stated once

This course follows Student Rule 7 exactly and adds nothing to it. The part worth knowing now is Student Rule 7.5: absences totaling more than 20% of scheduled class meetings or required instructional activities are considered unreasonable, and exceeding that — excused or unexcused — can carry a failing grade where the absences substantially prevent you completing what the course requires. Some of what happens in a studio cannot be made up afterward, which is what makes the 20% real here rather than theoretical.

Telling me is almost always enough. An excused absence needs only that you let me know about it; a planned unexcused absence needs only that you tell me in advance.

Why the outcomes are worded the way they are

The verbs in the learning outcomes are not decoration. They come from Bloom's taxonomy, the ordinary vocabulary for levels of learning:

Why the outcomes are worded the way they are
LevelWhat it asks of you
1 · RememberRecite the state capitals. Recall, and nothing beyond it.
2 · UnderstandSay why a thing works the way it does.
3 · ApplyYou know the tool names, you know which one to use and when, and you use it.
4 · AnalyzeLook at somebody's output and see that the densities are wrong.
5 · EvaluateWeigh competing approaches to the same problem against each other.
6 · CreateBuild an approach that did not exist before.

Most GIS coursework, including the labs, is assessed at level 3. This course is built to be assessed at 4, 5 and 6, which is why an outcome here says evaluate or defend where another course would say use.
