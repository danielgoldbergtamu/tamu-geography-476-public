How the session runs

Restate the client question, then read a few out

Write each of the two client questions in your own words, one sentence each. Not a summary of the project page, and not the question copied out — your sentence, saying what the client actually wants to know.

You will get it wrong in a specific and useful way, which is the point of doing it before you choose.

Read a few restatements out, and correct the ones that drifted

Four or five get read out. The room is not judging the writing; it is looking for drift — where the restatement quietly became a different question from the one the client asked.

There are four kinds of drift, and between them they account for nearly everything that gets read out.

Read a few restatements out, and correct the ones that drifted
The driftWhat it sounds likeWhy it matters
Drift to the method"Map the spread of screwworm using livestock movement data"The client did not ask for a map. They asked what could plausibly explain the movement, and a map is one possible answer
Drift to the easy half"Measure how fast African cities are growing"That is the first clause of a three-clause question. The clauses about infrastructure strain, foreign influence and transferability are the part that is hard, and the part the client cares about
Drift to certainty"Find out how the screwworm got to Zavala County"The question asks what evidence supports competing explanations. Rewriting it as one answer removes the analysis before it starts
Drift to the data you already imagined"Analyze the satellite imagery for urban expansion"You have chosen your data before you have understood the question. This is the most common one and the hardest to see in your own sentence

The test for whether a restatement drifted. Read it to somebody and ask: if I answered exactly this sentence, would the client have what they asked for? If the honest answer is "part of it", name which part is missing — that missing part is usually where the interesting work is.

Correcting a drifted restatement takes one sentence. Discovering in October that you have spent six weeks answering an easier question takes the rest of the semester.
