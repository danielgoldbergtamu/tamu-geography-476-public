0. The ask

let's close this log and start another one for the next missing slide without real data.

The next specimen in the client deck is SQ5, slide 14: "Where are livestock markets and aggregation points?"

1. Why this question is different from SQ3

SQ3 asked where the cattle ARE and was answered with a density surface: a continuous field, modelled, comparable across the border. SQ5 asks where the cattle are BROUGHT TOGETHER, and that is a point layer of named facilities with an owner, a licence number and a front gate.

The distinction matters for a screwworm question specifically. A density surface tells you where the exposure is spread; an aggregation point tells you where animals from many different places stand next to each other on the same day. For a parasite that travels on an animal, the second is the mechanism and the first is only the background.

It also changes what "no data" means. A missing density value can be modelled from covariates. A missing auction barn cannot be modelled at all - either somebody publishes the list or the map has a hole in it that no cleverness fills.

2. What I proposed, before finding out what I could reach

Written down before the first fetch, so that the record shows what I intended rather than only what I managed.

United States

  • TAHC licensed livestock markets. The Texas Animal Health Commission licenses livestock markets under Texas Agriculture Code ch. 146. The licence list is the authoritative Texas layer: a market that sells cattle in Texas is on it or it is operating illegally.
  • USDA AMS Livestock Market News. A subset of markets report receipts to AMS. This is the only published THROUGHPUT anywhere in the problem.
  • Texas Department of Agriculture livestock export pens. The border pens where Mexican cattle were held and inspected before the ports shut.

Mexico

  • SENASICA TIF establishments (Tipo Inspeccion Federal) - federally inspected slaughter and processing plants.
  • SIAP municipal Asacrificado - head slaughtered per municipality, already on disk at _Data/cattle/siap_cierre_pecuario_municipal_2025.csv from WGE-007. This is a fallback that does not depend on any facility list existing.

Technique. Geocode to points; build catchments weighted by the WGE-007 cattle density surface and the WGE-006/SQ4 road network; rank each market by the number of DISTINCT ORIGINS that converge on it rather than by throughput.

The reason for ranking on convergence rather than throughput is not elegance. It is that throughput is mostly unpublished. A ranking by throughput would silently drop every market that does not report to AMS, and the drop would look like a finding rather than like a missing column.

4. The American register, and how it was actually obtained

The plan named the TAHC licence list. Searching for it turned up something better: APHIS's Federally Approved Livestock Markets - the national register of markets approved for INTERSTATE movement under the traceability rule. Better for three reasons. It is national, so New Mexico and Arizona come with it. Its membership criterion is exactly the population a disease question cares about: markets a federal inspection regime knows by name. And it is updated nightly.

The page publishes no file. It embeds a Tableau dashboard on publicdashboards.dl.usda.gov, and the page's own text says the data is "updated nightly" without offering any way to have it.

Appending .csv to the Tableau view path returned the records behind the picture. 5,008 rows, 1,162 markets, 45 states, one row per market-and-species approval, 1.29 MB, downloaded through the browser into _Inbox and filed to _Data/markets/. Filtered to the Bovine approval: 941 markets, 54 of them in Texas, 11 in New Mexico, 8 in Arizona.

This is the third dashboard in four logs that turned out to be sitting on a plain file. It is worth writing the general lesson down rather than the specific trick: a dashboard is a renderer, and a renderer has to be given something. Tableau hands over summary data at <view path>.csv; Power BI answers a query endpoint; a DataTables grid is fed by a JSON call the network tab will show you. Learning those three removes most of the sentences that begin "the data is not downloadable".

What the list does not have

A coordinate. It publishes a street address and a city and no point at all.

5. Geocoding without a geocoder

Every geocoding service is refused from both shells, so the coordinates had to be built from files. tools/markets/geocode_markets.py joins each market's PREM CITY to published place files in four passes, weakest last, and records on every market which pass located it so a reader can throw away the weak ones:

exact 882 Census Gazetteer place, same state norm 8 after normalising punctuation and Ft./Mt./St. gnis 38 USGS populated place - NOT a Census place fuzzy 6 difflib within the state, cutoff 0.88 manual 5 corrected city name, recorded with a reason none 2

The GNIS pass is the one worth arguing for. A third of the misses are real communities that are not INCORPORATED, and no amount of fuzzy matching against a list of cities will ever find them. Raywood, Texas is a crossroads with a sale barn on it. It exists, the market exists, and the Census place file has never heard of either.

The five manual fixes are corrected city names, not typed coordinates, so they still resolve through the same published file as every other market and can be checked the same way. A hand-typed lat/lon is a number with no source. Two of the five are worth naming:

  • Douglan, AZ is a transposition of Douglas. Douglas is the only port on the entire southern border currently open to Mexican cattle. Of all 941 records this was the one it would have been least acceptable to drop.
  • Stearns, MN is a county written into the city field. The market's own name is Belgrade Livestock Market, and GeoNames places Belgrade in Stearns County. The two agree, so the county name is a filing error and not a second opinion.

Two markets are left unlocated and stay unlocated. OH176 has an empty city field and a name matching three different Ohio towns; VA1021 is in a community absent from both place files. Choosing for either would be a coin toss printed as a fact. Both are in content/projects/market_geocode_fixes.yaml under unresolved, with the reason.

Coordinates are city centroids, not front gates, and the figure says so.

6. The Mexican half, which turned out to be the stronger half

The announcement predicted this would be the weak side. That prediction was wrong and the record keeps it.

SENASICA publishes a Listado de rastros y/o centros de sacrificio, updated 1 May 2025: a 51-page PDF, parsed with pdfplumber into 745 facilities, 727 of which take cattle, across 30 states.

It publishes capacity. Every row carries an installed capacity and an average monthly slaughter figure in head. The APHIS register publishes no throughput for American auction markets at all. On the one measure the two countries could have shared, it is the American file that is silent.

The capacity cells are written for a human - "1,500 (bovinos), 2,000 (porcinos)" on a multi-species plant, a bare "350" on a single-species one. The parser takes the number in front of the species when the species is named, and a bare number only when the plant handles one species. Ninety-three plants are left blank rather than divided. A bare number on a multi-species plant is genuinely ambiguous, and apportioning it would be inventing a figure and attributing it to SENASICA.

Located against GeoNames in two passes: 556 by named locality, 175 by municipio centroid, 14 unlocated - and not one of the fourteen is in a border state.

The sentence in the title

"...de los que se tiene conocimiento, por parte de los gobiernos de los estados..." - the plants the state governments know about. The document does not claim completeness. Neither does this map, and that sentence travels with the layer.

7. Ranking on something that exists

The obvious ranking variable is throughput, and throughput is unpublished on the American side. Ranking on it anyway would have silently deleted every market that does not report to USDA AMS, and the deletion would have looked like a finding instead of a missing column.

So tools/markets/rank_markets.py ranks on proximity instead. Every GLW4 cattle cell is assigned to the nearest approved facility in its own country, and a facility's score is the herd assigned to it, cut at 100 air miles - the same 160.9 km that defines the interior enforcement zone on the SQ4b map, so both figures are cut with the same knife.

It claims: this many cattle live nearer to this than to anything else. It does not: this many cattle pass through it. Nobody knows that.

A mistake worth the space

The first version separated the two countries with latitude rules. The Rio Grande runs from 25.9 N to 31.8 N through several deep bends and no combination of horizontal cuts follows it. Worse, the rules handed every Mexican cell north of 25.9 N to American markets - which is precisely the error a map about cross-border movement exists not to make.

It was caught by reading the printed totals: 43.8 million head allocated to US markets from a window that stopped at 40.5 N was not obviously wrong on its own, and only looked wrong beside the Mexican number. The split is now done with the actual country polygons, and the wrong version is written into the source rather than deleted.

The result

US: 88,594,384 head in country; 86,069,802 allocated; 2,524,581 (2.8%) beyond MX: 33,479,274 head in country; 33,442,177 allocated; 37,096 (0.1%) beyond

The top American market is Hereford, Texas, 958,107 head, then Dodge City, Tulia, Syracuse, Texhoma, Dalhart. Hereford is in Deaf Smith County - one of the three counties WGE-007's first frame cut off and had to go back for. Two methods built from different data put the centre of gravity in the same place.

And the border count, which is the slide

Of 941 approved cattle markets, eight lie within 100 air miles of the Mexican border. Four are within 12 km of it: El Paso TX, Columbus NM, Santa Teresa NM, Douglas AZ.

Set that against the reopening register already in this repo from SQ4: USDA opened Douglas on 24 August and named Santa Teresa and Columbus as the two to be evaluated next. Every port in the phased reopening has a federally approved cattle market sitting on it.

East of El Paso the picture inverts. The nearest approved market to the border anywhere in Texas is San Angelo, 207 km - 129 air miles - inland, across about 1,900 km of river. On the Mexican side of that same stretch, 19 cattle slaughter facilities sit within 100 air miles and eight within 25 km of the line: Acuna 3 km, Nuevo Laredo 3 km, Reynosa 5 km, Ciudad Juarez 5 km.

The aggregation infrastructure runs up to the water on one side and stops a hundred and thirty miles short of it on the other. That lands directly on SQ4b's point about the ground between the river and the checkpoints being the part nothing counts, and sharpens it: an animal crossing that stretch does not pass an approved market either.

8. Drawing it, and three things the drawing caught

The two countries are drawn in different shapes as well as different colours, because they are different instruments and a reader must not be able to slide from one to the other - and because shape is what survives grayscale and a bad projector (R124, WCAG 1.4.1).

The gold squares were failing the projector test. Gold at 62 percent over the land tone is about 2.2:1 against it, under WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1. The square's OUTLINE is what carries the boundary, so the outline is what has to pass - and the first edge, #5E4410 at 0.55 pt, measured 2.8:1 after the simulated projector wash (out = px*0.52 + 112). Darkened to #3A2A08 at 0.7 pt: 3.4:1. The same test that rescued the W02 county boundaries.

The findings column ran off the bottom of the slide. Fixed by measuring the wrapped text first and choosing the leading from it, rather than fixing the leading and discovering the overflow in the render. This is the third time that exact mistake has been made in this repo - twice in evidence_slide - and it is always the same shape: size the container, then find out what was supposed to go in it.

A gate had been broken for three logs. language_check was carrying 94 British spellings - kilometre, metres, colour, modelled, licence, judgement - almost all introduced by WGE-005, 006 and 007, because the gate had never been run. Fixed across 28 files. One correction needed a second look: disc to disk, where the function meant a circular neighbourhood of raster cells rather than storage. The rename applied to the definition and all five call sites together, so the code still runs, and "disk" is defensible for that shape.

9. The landmine, and the worse fix for it

tools/slides/figs_plans.py draws the SPECIMEN maps. When sq03 was promoted to real yesterday, the register and the alt text were updated - and sq03() was left in that file's draw list.

Anyone running the specimen generator would have overwritten the real cattle density map with a picture of stand-in values, and the register's alt text would still have said it was real. Not a broken build. A green one, with a false map in it. The same was about to be true of sq05.

The guard now reads the register. But the first version of the guard was itself wrong: it skipped every plan marked map_status: real, which silently stopped drawing sq01 and sq02 - both of which are real, are drawn in that same file from the chronology register, and write to w02_ rather than plan_. Two figures lost to fix an overwrite of two others.

The working test is about the filename, not the status: skip a plan only when its figure: field names the exact file this script would write over. Both versions are in the source, the wrong one described.

10. Everything is on disk, and it rebuilds

Per the standing instruction that everything used, downloaded or created is stored here:

_Data/markets/aphis_approved_livestock_markets_2026-09-02.csv 1,292,507 _Data/markets/senasica_listado_rastros_2025-05-01.pdf 1,561,797 _Data/markets/2024_Gaz_place_national.zip 1,221,402 _Data/markets/DomesticNames_National_Text.zip 38,581,543 _Data/markets/geonames_MX.zip 14,872,441 _Data/markets/gnis_populated_places.tsv 11,598,811 (derived)

tools/markets/fetch_inputs.py verifies all six, --urls prints the exact address each came from, and --extract rebuilds the derived one. The archives are committed as downloaded and read out of the ZIP at run time, so the file on disk is the file the agency served, byte for byte, with nothing edited in between. Only the GNIS extract is excluded from version control, because storing a file beside the archive it was unpacked from is duplication that can disagree with itself.

_Inbox is empty of everything this log put there; the consumed downloads are in _to_delete/2026-09-02_inbox_consumed/ (N16).

The whole pipeline was re-run end to end and the three derived CSVs came back byte-identical (md5sum -c: three OK). That is the check that matters: a result that cannot be reproduced from the files on disk is a result that only exists in a transcript.
