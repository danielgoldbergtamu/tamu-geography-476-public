Both projects reached this course as a request from somebody outside it, written in a format you have probably never seen. This page is those two requests, restated. Read them before you do Engagement 2.

Why this page exists, and what it is not

The two requests below did not arrive as project descriptions. They arrived as Key Intelligence Questions — KIQs — which is how the intelligence community hands work to the people who are going to do it. A KIQ names the question somebody in a position to act actually needs answered. It does not tell you the method, the data, the software, or the shape of the answer. Working out all of that is the job.

That is the format, and the format is the point. You are going to spend a career receiving work in whatever form the person asking happens to use, and the first skill is reading a request written by somebody who does not know what you can do, and does not know what they left out.

What this page is not. It is not the full client packet, and it is not a work plan. Each request below has been restated in a common format so the two projects can be read side by side. The originals are the clients' own documents; you will see the relevant ones as the projects develop.

One thing has been deliberately left out of both. Each client also sent a list of the sub-questions they think follow from their main question. You are not getting those yet, because working out what a question breaks down into is the thing Engagement 2 is asking you to attempt. You will see the clients' own lists afterward, and comparing yours against theirs is worth more than reading theirs first.

Request 1 — New World screwworm

Requested by: the U.S. Department of Agriculture, through the GEOINT program at Texas A&M.

Situation. New World screwworm (Cochliomyia hominivorax) is a parasitic fly whose larvae infest living tissue. Its reemergence in Mexico, and subsequent detections in the United States, create a problem that involves animal movement, disease transmission, transportation networks, geography, surveillance, and uncertainty — all at once.

The question. What geographic, environmental, livestock-network, and surveillance factors could plausibly contribute to the movement of screwworm-infested cattle from southern Mexico toward South Texas, and what evidence supports competing explanations for the June 2026 detections in Zavala County?

What the client wants at the end. An intelligence estimate, not a map. The distinction matters and it is not a formality: a map shows where things are, an estimate says what is most plausibly happening, on what evidence, with how much confidence, and what would have to be true for it to be wrong.

Constraint the client stated. Publicly available information only.

Request 2 — Urbanization in West Africa

Requested by: the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, through the GEOINT program at Texas A&M.

Situation. Several large African cities are being built very quickly. The rate and the financing of that construction are not fully explained by the infrastructure programs already known to be operating in the region.

The question. Who is funding this urban expansion beyond the already-understood sources; is the expansion sustainable given the region's projected youth population; and are there third-party interests involved that a U.S. policymaker would want to know about?

Where to look. The client suggested Abuja or Lagos as the study area.

What the client wants at the end. An assessment a policymaker could act on — which means the finding has to survive somebody asking "how do you know that?"

What to notice before you start

Neither request tells you how. No data sources, no software, no method, no boundary, no date range, no definition of the words doing the most work — "plausibly", "sustainable", "third-party interests", "surveillance coverage". Every one of those is a decision somebody has to make, and if you do not make it deliberately, your tool will make it for you and not mention that it did.

Neither request is complete, and that is normal. A client asks for what they want, not for what you need. The gap between those two is where most of the work in this course lives.

Both questions have an answer the client is probably expecting. Notice that too. It is the most dangerous thing on this page.
