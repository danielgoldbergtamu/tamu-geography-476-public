0. The ask

can we close the log and start another one. In the new one, i would like us review the analysis we have completed so far in the other logs, now with the knowledge of the incidence data we obtained through our deep dig. Do we have updated that we can make now that we have these data?

WGE-009 is closed. This log is a review, not a new question: go back through what WGE-005 to WGE-009 concluded and ask which conclusions the municipio-level incidence capture can now sharpen, overturn, or leave alone.

1. First, the honest inventory of what the dig actually produced

_Data/incidence/senasica_powerbi_municipio_capture.json holds 143 matrix rows and four raw querydata bodies from the SENASICA Casos activos page, report dated 30 August 2026.

What that is:

  • Active cases only. 1,941 of 40,392 cumulative — five percent of the epidemic, and a snapshot rather than a history.
  • Ten states, complete within themselves. Zacatecas, Nayarit, Oaxaca, Yucatan, Puebla, Hidalgo, Chiapas, San Luis Potosi, Queretaro, Quintana Roo and Jalisco. Zacatecas came out as all 43 of its municipios, from Pinos at 28 down to a tail of ones.
  • No border state. Coahuila, Nuevo Leon, Tamaulipas, Chihuahua and Sonora are not in the captured set, because the matrix is sorted by descending active cases and those states have few.

2. The distinction WGE-009 got right, and the one it did not have to make

WGE-009 refused this data, and the reason was correct: the matrix is sorted by the response variable, so the ten states that came out are the ten states with the most active cases. Correlating anything against a case dataset that only exists where the cases are is circular.

But that argument is about the STATES, not the municipios. Within a captured state the municipio list is complete — every municipio in Zacatecas with an active case is in the file, and the ones with none are absent because they have none, not because the scroll bar stopped.

So the bias is entirely at the state level, and the fix is the one this project has now used twice: condition on the confounder. A within-state analysis, where every comparison happens between municipios of the same state and no comparison crosses a state line, is legitimate on exactly this data.

That is the same move as the cattle-weighted null in WGE-009 section 1 and the border-matched null in its addendum. Three times in two days the answer has been "stratify on the thing that selected your sample".

3. What this makes newly answerable

WGE-009 named its own limitation in writing:

a null result across 30 states cannot rule out a relationship at the scale of a single municipio, which is the resolution nobody publishes.

That resolution is now partly published, and the limitation is testable. The question this log has to answer first is therefore not a new one — it is the one WGE-009 said it could not reach.

4. Second attempt at the report, and where it stops

Before using the partial capture, the two untried techniques from the dig note were tried, because 40,392 cumulative cases at municipio resolution would change what is possible far more than 1,941 active ones.

Attempt 1 - hook installed before the report renders. The fetch hook was installed on the top document immediately after navigation and left running for thirteen seconds while the report loaded, with an interval re-hooking the iframe as it appeared. Nothing was captured during load. This confirms the dig note's diagnosis rather than contradicting it: the report renders from cache and issues no query on a plain load.

Attempt 2 - switch to the Casos acumulados page. The page button is a visual-container and .click() on it does not navigate. The report stayed on Casos activos.

Attempt 3 - force a re-query by expanding. Clicking an expand button captured nothing this time either, and then no collapsed rows remained to click: Power BI had cached the expansions from the earlier session in this browser profile. The first dig's successful captures came from expanding states that had never been expanded before. So the technique works exactly once per state per profile, which is worth knowing and is not in the note.

Stopped here. The remaining routes are a real mouse wheel over the visual - which needs the report canvas brought on screen first, and the canvas is larger than the window - and replaying the query with the state restriction removed, which needs a REQUEST body that this attempt did not manage to capture. Both are recorded in 06_Working_Notes/border_data_dig.md. The cumulative page is still out of reach, and the analysis below uses the 1,941 active cases.

5. Salvaging the capture, and what it cost

The rows arrived in the order the matrix was scrolled and expanded, not in state-then-municipio order. The file holds Zacatecas and its 42 municipios contiguously, then three state headers in a row, then ninety municipio rows belonging to those three states in no recoverable order, then seven more collapsed headers with nothing under them.

Assigning each municipio to the last header seen gives Yucatan 276 active cases against a published 118 - a number that would have looked entirely plausible on a slide.

So attribution is done two ways and checked against a third:

  1. the leading contiguous block, by order, which is trustworthy exactly once because nothing else had appeared yet;
  2. the remainder by name, against GeoNames ADM2 restricted to the states actually captured, with Zacatecas removed from the candidate set - which is also what clears the ambiguity, since Guadalupe, Benito Juarez and Villa Hidalgo are municipios in Zacatecas and elsewhere;
  3. every state's municipios then summed and compared to its active count in border_movement.yaml, which was captured independently off the APHIS map, so the match is a cross-check rather than a tautology.

Two states reconcile exactly, and only two.

Zacatecas 42 municipios 191 active cases matches published 191 Oaxaca 64 municipios 135 active cases matches published 135

Nayarit harvested 122 of 141 DROPPED Yucatan harvested 91 of 118 DROPPED

The other seven states were never expanded and contribute nothing. tools/municipio/parse_capture.py prints the reconciliation on every run, so the drop is visible rather than silent.

6. The test WGE-009 said it could not reach

WGE-009 wrote its own limitation down: "a null result across 30 states cannot rule out a relationship at the scale of a single municipio, which is the resolution nobody publishes." For two states it now can.

Unit: the municipio. Response: active cases, zero where absent - real zeros, because the matrix lists only municipios that have cases. Exposure: SIAP 2025 bovine live-cattle production in tonnes, which is production and not inventory and is a proxy, stated as one. Predictor: distance from the municipio's GeoNames ADM2 centroid to the nearest cattle slaughter facility. Every comparison inside one state; none across a state line.

Zacatecas 42 of 58 municipios have cases observed 11.3 km null 11.7 km p 0.69 smallest difference the test could resolve 1.8 km Oaxaca 62 of 529 municipios have cases observed 51.7 km null 52.7 km p 0.77 smallest difference the test could resolve 6.2 km

The answer does not change, and this time the power is not in doubt. In Zacatecas the test could have resolved a difference of 1.8 km and found 0.4 km. That is a much sharper instrument than anything in WGE-009, and it is pointed at the exact scale WGE-009 conceded it could not see.

Two rank correlations, for completeness:

Zacatecas cases vs distance rho 0.092 p 0.56 rate vs distance rho -0.019 p 0.89 Oaxaca cases vs distance rho -0.001 p 0.99 rate vs distance rho -0.117 p 0.007

One of those six is significant, and it is the only positive signal anywhere in this exercise: in Oaxaca, case rate per tonne of cattle rises slightly as distance to a facility falls. It is in the direction the original hypothesis predicted. It is also rho = -0.12 across 529 municipios, which explains about one percent of the variance, on a production proxy, in one state. It is reported and it is not built on.

7. The uncomfortable check, and it belongs in the review

Every null model in WGE-009 and in this log is weighted by cattle, on the premise that cases occur where the cattle are. That premise is testable at this resolution, and if it fails the weighting is not a correction but a distortion. So it was tested:

Zacatecas cattle vs cases rho 0.265 p 0.044 Oaxaca cattle vs cases rho 0.113 p 0.009

Positive and significant in both, so the premise holds - and it holds weakly. Cattle production explains roughly seven percent of the variance in case counts in Zacatecas and about one percent in Oaxaca.

That does not invalidate the cattle-weighted null; a weak true relationship is still the right thing to condition on, and conditioning on it can only have made those tests more conservative. But it does mean the cattle surface is a much weaker explanation of where cases are than the framing of WGE-009 implied, and it is consistent with that log's own state-level finding that cattle head against case count came out at rho 0.21, p 0.26. Two resolutions, same weak signal. The thing that moves cases around is not the thing that moves cattle around.

8. The review, log by log

The instruction was to go back over what the earlier logs concluded. Each is listed with what the municipio data does to it, including the ones it does nothing to - because "no change" is a result of the review and not an absence of one.

WGE-005 — the transport network and the ports (slides 10, 11)

No change, and no change was possible. That log is about freight volumes at ports of entry and the ground between the river and the interior checkpoints. The municipio capture covers Zacatecas and Oaxaca, which are 700 and 1,500 km from the border respectively. Nothing here can speak to it.

WGE-006 — the crossing suitability model (slides 12, 13)

No change, same reason. The model scores 411 km of the Rio Grande between Del Rio and Laredo. The captured states do not touch it.

Worth stating plainly because it is the sort of thing that gets fudged: new data that is about the same disease is not new data about the same question.

WGE-007 — where the cattle are (slide 9)

No change to the map. One thing added underneath it. The cattle surface is unaffected. But this log tested, for the first time, whether case counts track cattle at a fine scale, and found rho 0.265 and 0.113 - positive, significant, weak. That is a footnote to SQ3 rather than a correction of it, and it is recorded in section 7.

WGE-008 — livestock markets and aggregation points (slide 14)

No change. The finding is about the 1,900 km of border with no approved US market inside 100 air miles, and about the asymmetry between the two national registers. Two interior Mexican states cannot test it.

The facility layer itself was used here, as the predictor, which is the only way this log touches WGE-008 at all.

WGE-009 — do the cases sit where the animals are gathered (slides 15, 16)

This is the one that changes, and it changes by being strengthened.

WGE-009's stated limit was that a null across 30 states could not rule out a relationship at municipio scale, "which is the resolution nobody publishes". Two things about that sentence are now wrong: the resolution is partly published, and the test has been run. In both states the answer is the same as at state level, and in Zacatecas the test could resolve 1.8 km.

So the correction to make is not to the finding. It is to the limitation - the slide currently claims an escape hatch that has since been closed, and leaving it there would understate the result. That text lives in content/projects/incidence_relationship.yaml, is read by the figure and by the deck, and is amended in section 9.

The one thing that survives as a genuine limit: both tested states are interior. A municipio-scale test has still never been run anywhere near the border, which is where every operational question in this project actually lives.

The methodological thread

Three selection problems in three days, one answer each time:

WGE-009 cases and markets are both where the cattle are weight the null by cattle WGE-009 detections are near the border, markets are not match the null on border distance WGE-010 the captured states are the states with most cases compare only within a state

The pattern is worth more than any of the three findings, and it is the thing to teach: a null model corrects for what you put in it, and the work is deciding what to put in.

9. What was actually changed, and what was left alone

Amended, because it had become false. Slide 15 and its register said a null across 30 states "cannot rule out a relationship at the scale of a single municipio, which is the resolution nobody publishes." Both halves of that are now wrong. The text in content/projects/incidence_relationship.yaml and the matching limits in content/projects/screwworm_research_plans.yaml now say what was actually done and what survives - that both tested states are interior, and no municipio-scale test has been run near the border.

A caveat that is no longer true understates the result, and leaving it in place would have been a quiet form of hedging. This is the whole argument for keeping the words in a register: one edit, and the figure, the deck and the speaker notes all say the new thing.

Added to the figure, in four lines: the municipio-scale retest and its resolution. The figure's reading column sizes itself from the text, so it absorbed the extra paragraph and dropped from 7.4 pt to 6.5 pt without anything running off the bottom - which is the third time that measure-then-size rule has paid for itself.

Not changed: WGE-005, WGE-006, WGE-007 and WGE-008. Two interior states cannot speak to a border question, and section 8 says so for each one individually rather than leaving the reader to infer it.

No new slide. The result is a strengthening of an existing claim, not a new claim, and it belongs where the claim already is.
