What the data turned out to be

Every source that was tried, including the ones that gave nothing. A source that yielded nothing is a finding, not a gap in the record.

What the data turned out to be
SourceWhat it gave
Texas Animal Health Commission, emergency pagegave what was needed — Nine confirmed detections across five counties, each with a date and a species, plus the twelve-county quarantine list.
APHIS confirmed casesgave nothing usable — Confirms in its own words that per-county case data exists only inside an embedded dashboard. No county list, no export.

What does not exist

  • A per-county time series of confirmed cases. 47 detections are counted; 13 counties can be named from published text. The other 34 have no county anywhere public.
  • Sub-county geography. Every zone is described as covering PARTS of the counties named. Whole counties are drawn and the figure says so.

Where the sources disagreed

Reported rather than resolved. When two sources disagree and neither is obviously wrong, the disagreement is part of the result.

  • Have any Texas counties been released from movement restriction?
  • Yes — zones 02.A and 03 released 31 August (trade press reporting an APHIS action)
  • TAHC's own emergency page names no released counties

Defects in the sources, quoted rather than repaired

  • A confirmation date given as "June 31", which does not exist. Stored as a named county with no date. Repairing it would have meant guessing which of two plausible corrections was meant.
  • "Parts of 22 Texas counties" with no list — a count without members. Stored as a count with a null member list, so the 1 August panel prints "zone list not published" rather than drawing an empty zone layer.

What was NOT built, and why

Read this section first if you read nothing else. The deliverable that was refused is the one you would most likely have produced.

  • A choropleth of confirmed cases per county. Thirteen counties shaded, 241 white, captioned "documented cases" — and 34 real detections rendered as absence. Every shaded county would have been correct. It would have been wrong entirely in what it omitted, and a reader could not have detected that from the artifact. It is the error W02 teaches on the preceding slide.
  • Built instead: Two layers labeled as different kinds of fact — zones and named cases — with the count of each printed under every panel and a footer stating in bold that an uncolored county means no source published a county.

What no check would have caught

The rules and gates this course runs on, and why each one would have passed the version that was wrong.

  • R40 — the figure would have been registered
  • R60 — it would have had alternative text
  • R94 — it would have fitted its box
  • the AI Review Log — a map wrong by omission contains no claim to reject

What went wrong while building it

  • Every panel caption rendered invisible; the build reported success. Captions written in data coordinates on a projected axes landed a meter above the equator, outside the extent. A label off the edge of a map is not an error to matplotlib. Caught by looking at the rendered image.

What to take from it

  • The mechanical work — geometry, join, projection, styling — took the least time and was never where the risk was.
  • The join was verified against the geometry's own names BEFORE anything was drawn. A text-key join is the most common silent failure in this work, and checking it converts a wrong map into an error message.
  • A count and a location are different records, published by different bodies at different cadences, and they diverge. The divergence is the finding.
  • No gate in this project would have caught the wrong version. The only thing between the easy map and the honest one was somebody asking what the white counties meant.

What this means for the job you are about to apply for

The parts a first-year analyst is usually hired to do are the parts that took the least time. What was left is the part that is not automatable: knowing that the deliverable being asked for was the wrong deliverable, and being able to say why. Recorded because the instructor asked for it to be discussed with the class rather than implied.

The map itself

Shown here rather than only linked, because a map is the one artifact where seeing it IS understanding it.

What this map shows Four small maps of Texas, one per date, showing counties in two treatments that mean two different things and that differ in PATTERN as well as color, so the distinction survives color blindness and grayscale printing. A hatched gold county is under a movement restriction; a solid maroon county with a heavy dark outline has a confirmed case that somebody published by name. A drawn legend on the figure says both. On 3 June 2026 one county is maroon, Zavala, with one detection recorded and no restriction zone yet. On 12 June, five counties are maroon and twelve are gold in a band across the Hill Country and the border, with nine detections recorded - the only date where the two layers agree, because every detection was named, dated and located. On 1 August, eleven counties are maroon and no county is gold, because forty-four detections had been recorded and the restriction list was reported only as "parts of 22 counties" without naming them. On 26 August, thirteen counties are maroon and twenty-three gold, spreading west into the Trans-Pecos and south to the Rio Grande, with forty-seven detections recorded. The figure establishes that the two records diverge: the number of detections rises far faster than the number of counties anybody can name, and by the last date thirty-four of forty-seven detections have no county in any public text, because the per-case detail is held in a dashboard that publishes no county list. A footer states that a county with no color means no source published a county rather than no cases, that zones and cases are published by different bodies and do not nest, that every zone covers only parts of the counties named while whole counties are drawn, and that the projection is Albers equal area.

What this map shows A map and a timeline, side by side, plotted from the project's own chronology register rather than drawn. The map is a Lambert conformal conic projection of Mexico, Central America and the southern United States. Seven numbered points mark the first confirmed detection in each country and state, joined by a dashed line: 1 Panama, 2 Costa Rica, 3 Nicaragua, 4 Honduras, 5 Chiapas in southern Mexico, 6 Guatemala, and 7 Zavala County in south Texas. The points march from the Panamanian isthmus to the Texas border. A callout records what followed the last of them - 45 confirmed detections by 10 August 2026, 44 in Texas and one in New Mexico. Beneath the map, the same seven points sit on a time axis from 2022 to 2026, and the intervals between them visibly shorten from left to right: almost a year from Panama to Costa Rica, then Nicaragua, Honduras, Chiapas and Guatemala inside eighteen months, then Texas. The map establishes that the spread is northward and continuous; the timeline establishes that it is accelerating. To the right, the five most recent response actions in date order - a phased reopening of Mexican cattle imports, two sterile fly facilities announced, a port of entry reopening, and the first infested-zone releases - establishing that the countermeasures sit inside the same timeline as the spread. A footer names the projection, states that the points are country and county centroids rather than the location of any animal, says that the dashed line is a sequence and not a route, and gives the date the record ends.

A vector PDF of the same figure is in the table below. Download that one if you need to zoom — the panels draw some counties a few millimeters across and a magnified PNG turns to mush.
