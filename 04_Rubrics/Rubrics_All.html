Why these are written the way they are

Every rating level states an observable, checkable condition rather than a quality adjective — not “demonstrates critical thinking” but “rejects at least two AI critiques, each with a stated reason referencing specific content.”

This serves three readers. A student can read the rubric and know what to do. The instructor grades faster and more consistently. And where AI is used as a grading aid, observable criteria make a first pass reliable enough to be worth reviewing.

On grading: AI is one tool among several. Every grade is reviewed and set by the instructor, who reads the work. No student is graded by a machine alone.

Note — rubric placement (tracker R-1)

All rubrics are consolidated here while they are being drafted, which keeps them consistent and reviewable as a set. Each probably belongs on its own assignment page eventually. Revisit once the wording is settled.

AI Competency

Rating levels — AI Competency
LevelWhat the grader must be able to point at
Exceeds Mastery (4)AI Review Log complete and specific. Names the tools and personas used, quotes or summarizes what each critique actually said, and shows revisions traceable to it. Rejects two or more critiques with reasons that reference specific content — and at least one rejection identifies something the AI got factually or methodologically wrong. The transcript is attached and every claim the log makes can be found in it, including the opening prompt that states the rule for this assignment.
Mastery (3)AI Review Log complete. Personas named, critiques summarized, revisions traceable. At least one critique rejected with a stated reason. Transcript attached and consistent with the log.
Near Mastery (2)AI Review Log present but thin. Personas named without saying what they returned, or revisions asserted without being traceable, or rejections listed with no reason given — or the transcript is missing, so nothing in the log can be checked.
Below Mastery (1)AI Review Log present but does not evidence use — names a tool only, or the 'rejected' field is empty or says 'none' with no explanation.
No Evidence (0)No AI Review Log and no transcript submitted.

AI in Technical Practice

Rating levels — AI in Technical Practice
LevelWhat the grader must be able to point at
Exceeds Mastery (4)AI Method Log complete and specific. AI was used at two or more distinct stages of the technical work you own — and for every suggestion accepted, the log shows how it was verified: against documentation, against a known-good result, against a hand calculation, or by a test that would have failed if the suggestion were wrong. At least one AI proposal is identified as wrong, with the evidence that proved it wrong.
Mastery (3)AI Method Log complete. The stage of the workflow is named, the prompt and the context supplied are recorded, and at least one accepted suggestion is shown to have been independently verified rather than trusted.
Near Mastery (2)AI Method Log present but thin. The workflow stage is named without saying what was asked, or verification is asserted — "I checked it" — without showing what was checked against — or the transcript is missing, so nothing in the log can be checked.
Below Mastery (1)AI Method Log present but does not evidence technical use. Names a tool only, describes AI help with the writing rather than the work — which is permitted on project work and is simply not what this criterion grades — or records suggestions that were adopted with no verification of any kind.
No Evidence (0)No AI Method Log and no transcript submitted.

Length Discipline

Rating levels — Length Discipline
LevelWhat the grader must be able to point at
Exceeds Mastery (4)Hits the target exactly, and reads as though that length were chosen rather than imposed. Nothing is padded and nothing is truncated. The cuts that were made are invisible because what remains is the strongest version of the argument. A reader would not guess there was a limit.
Mastery (3)Hits the target. The content fits the space it was given, and the argument is complete within it.
Near Mastery (2)Close to the target, but the strain shows — visible padding, a section compressed to the point of vagueness, or an argument that stops rather than finishes. The length is being served instead of the reader.
Below Mastery (1)Meaningfully over or under the target. Running over means the writer did not decide what mattered; running short means they did not find enough to say, or stopped before they did.
No Evidence (0)The target was ignored, or padding was used to reach it.
